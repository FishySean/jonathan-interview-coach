## Core principles

- **Reject, disagree, and handle conflict without escalating** — demonstrated via short workplace dialogues (title: sandwich method).
- Pattern in the scripts: acknowledge / validate → clear boundary or disagreement → supportive redirect or shared goal.
- **Attack the problem, not the person** — when someone gets personal, name the impact and reset to working *with* them.
- Preserve relationship language: "I'm rooting for you," "proud of," "value working through disagreements with you, not against you."

## Interview philosophy

Useful for behavioral conflict / pushback / priority questions. Jonathan models calm firmness: empathy first, then "but," then collaborative next step — never raw refusal or counterattack alone.

## Evaluation criteria

Score conflict answers on:

1. **Acknowledgment** of the other person's pressure/intent
2. **Clear no / disagreement** without apology-spiral
3. **Shared goal or redirect** (quality, profit+volume, future expectations, reset)
4. **Tone** — peer, not victim or aggressor

## Common candidate mistakes

- Flat "no" with no acknowledgment.
- Agreeing to impossible timelines that cut quality without naming the tradeoff.
- Mocking bad ideas without redirecting creativity.
- Getting defensive when blamed, instead of transparent causes + alignment.
- Matching personal attacks with personal attacks.

## Follow-up questions

- "Where's the acknowledgment / the boundary / the shared goal in that answer?"
- "How do you say no without sounding unhelpful?"
- "When they made it personal, how did you reset to the problem?"

## Answer frameworks

**Implied sandwich (from examples):** warm recognition → firm pushback → constructive path forward.

**Shared-goal bridge phrases he uses:**
- "I know we both want…"
- "I'm sure you can…"
- "I'd like to take this opportunity to align…"
- "I'd like to reset, because I value working through disagreements with you…"

## Good vs bad examples

### Request dump: do my reports

**Ask (verbatim):**

> Hey John, I'm so busy today. Can you do these reports for me?

**Jonathan's response (verbatim):**

> Wow, I can tell you are slammed. Unfortunately, I can't help you with that since I have a full plate as well. But you're resourceful, I'm sure you'll find a way. And I'm rooting for you.

---

### Impossible deadline: finish tomorrow

**Ask (verbatim):**

> Change of plans. Instead of next week, I need you to finish the project tomorrow.

**Jonathan's response (verbatim):**

> I totally get the urgency. I want to get this done faster too. But with the current scope, delivering tomorrow means cutting quality. And I know we both want to deliver something that we're proud of. So I'm happy to discuss how we can do that.

---

### Bad idea: BOGO extreme

**Ask (verbatim):**

> We need to increase sales volume. I think we should do a Bodo deal. Buy one, get 10 free.

**Jonathan's response (verbatim):**

> You're so… creative. That's what I appreciate about you. But we have to keep our eye on profit and brand reputation as well. I'm sure you can direct your creativity towards an idea that supports both volume and profit, right?

---

### Blame: project late / unacceptable

**Ask (verbatim):**

> Why is this project late? This is unacceptable.

**Jonathan's response (verbatim):**

> I hear you. And I want to reassure you. Staying on track is my top priority. Transparently, the additional scope added over the past week has extended the timeline. I know there were good reasons for these additions. And in fact, I'd like to take this opportunity to align on future expectations.

---

### Personal attack: stop overreacting

**Ask (verbatim):**

> I didn't mean it that way. Stop overreacting. You need to relax.

**Jonathan's response (verbatim):**

> We respect each other, right? So I'm sure you didn't mean it that way. See, when you attacked me instead of the problem, it felt personal. And it made me uncomfortable. But I'd like to reset, because I value working through disagreements with you, not against you.
