## Core principles

- **Best way to become a manager: interview for it first** — don’t wait forever for boss/headcount.
- You don’t need a manager title handed to you — **go get it**; surface management **competencies**, not only titled experience.
- Real manager job essence: **delegate + track progress; develop talent; coach/support** — all showable without direct reports.
- Two proof points: **mentorship** + **leading without authority** (“invisible promotions”).
- Craft a **people-leadership philosophy** (from Julie Zhuo / “Julia Droz” ASR — *The Making of a Manager*): multipliers, trust first, leadership is learned — then tie real stories to it.
- Stop waiting for permission — the interview is already happening.

## Interview philosophy

Manager hiring reads transferable leadership evidence + coherent philosophy. Mentorship and influence-without-authority stories beat “I’ve never managed.”

## Evaluation criteria

- Mentorship evidence (onboard, review work, go-to person)
- Lead without authority (rally, align, cross-functional glue)
- Named philosophy + linked proof story
- Delegate/track/develop/coach mapped without title

## Common candidate mistakes

- Waiting for permission / headcount
- Thinking no direct reports = no management proof
- No summarized leadership philosophy

## Follow-up questions

- “Where have I mentored or been the go-to?”
- “Where did I lead without authority?”
- “Which philosophy (multiplier / trust / learned) fits — with what story?”

## Answer frameworks

### Proof stack

1. Surface competencies (delegate, develop, coach)  
2. Mentorship stories + lead-without-authority stories  
3. Pick philosophy → tie experience  

### Three philosophies (pick one)

1. Managers as multipliers  
2. Trust first  
3. Leadership is learned  

## Good vs bad examples

### Waiting vs interviewing for the title

**Teaching (verbatim):**

> The best way to become a manager is to interview for it first… you don't need to be given a manager title, you can go out and get a manager title yourself.

### Philosophy + story (verbatim)

> my leadership philosophy is that trust is earned, not deserved. When I mentored two junior analysts, I built trust by giving us a safe space every week where we went beyond tasks to focus on strategic thinking. One of them ended up leading their first project solo.

> I believe that even when you have formal authority as a manager, it is far better to influence without it. For example, in our Q4 lunch, I drove three teams to alignment, including more senior leaders. I multiplied my impact by removing roadblocks and supporting my team, not by giving orders.

*(ASR: “Q4 lunch” ≈ Q4 launch.)*
