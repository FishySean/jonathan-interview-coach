## Core principles

- ESL brains aren’t just slower — they’re **overwhelmed** (System 2 overuse).
- Native speakers live in **System 1** (autopilot) and switch to System 2 only for deep reasoning; many Chinese ESL pros do the **opposite**.
- Before communicating: **Does my performance depend on communicating carefully here?** If no → System 1; allow imperfect.
- ~**80% of daily work communication is low stakes**.
- Fluency improves with **10× daily practice** (<10 min/day, every day for a month).

## Interview philosophy

(Workplace ESL / confidence.) Perfect grammar on every Slack isn’t the job. Over-editing burns the brain. Practice speaking without pause/hesitation/grammar worry in tiny daily moments so System 1 becomes usable at work — including interviews where sounding natural beats sounding “perfect.”

## Evaluation criteria

- Low-stakes messages: System 1 OK (imperfect allowed)
- High-stakes (manager, presentations): System 2 intentional
- Daily practice: 10 moments × 30–60s clear natural English

## Common candidate mistakes

- Running System 2 on everything to “sound fluent/fast like a native.”
- Obsessing over perfect grammar on routine messages (wife asking Jonathan to review mid-day; later ChatGPT).
- No deliberate low-stakes speaking practice.

## Follow-up questions

Self-check:

- “Does my performance depend on communicating carefully here?”
- If no: “Am I giving myself permission to be imperfect?”

## Answer frameworks

### System 1 vs 2 (Kahneman)

- **System 1:** autopilot — e.g. mom asks 吃饭了吗? → 吃了.
- **System 2:** slow, intentional, hard — e.g. parallel park while dad watches.

### Decision rule

> Does my performance depend on communicating carefully here? If no, switch to system one. Give yourself permission to be imperfect.

### 10× daily practice

Pick 10 tiny moments (walk to lunch, elevator, groceries). For 30–60 seconds each, speak English as clearly and naturally as you can — **no pausing, no hesitation, no worrying about grammar or pronunciation**.
