## Core principles

- There are **four ways** to get a job (Core Four); most people are stuck on the first (mass apply to strangers).
- Every day you choose **who** to reach out to and **how**.
- A true referral = employee tells HM “I know this person and you should interview them because of XYZ” — not just a form.
- **Cold message** is terrifying/awkward and almost nobody does it — that’s why you stand out (30 minutes).
- Almost no Chinese professionals share expertise on LinkedIn; Western peers do — learning in public works.
- **Rule of 20:** every 20 days without results, move **20%** effort from applications into another box.

## Interview philosophy

Job search is a 2×2: know you / don’t know you × pitch one / pitch many. Rebalance away from the hardest, most competitive box.

## Evaluation criteria

Core Four coverage; quality of referrals (advocacy, not forms); cold outreach to decision makers; LinkedIn expertise sharing; flexible 20% reallocation.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Living only in mass applications (thousands of competitors, endless rejections).
- Treating oversaturated coffee-chat/referral theater as enough.
- Avoiding cold messages for a hundred reasons.
- Skipping LinkedIn sharing (esp. Chinese professionals).
- “I’m too busy applying” → never diversify channels.

## Follow-up questions

- “Which Core Four box am I in this week?”
- “After 20 days of weak results, what 20% will I move?”

## Answer frameworks

### Core Four grid

|  | Pitch one person | Pitch many |
|--|------------------|------------|
| **Know you** | Warm network / true referrals | LinkedIn sharing / learning in public |
| **Don’t know you** | Cold message to decision makers | Mass applications (where most people live) |

### Cold message (30 min)

1. List target companies.
2. Find decision makers (HM, directors, VPs, executives).
3. Send message using the template (template referenced but not fully dictated in transcript).

### Rule of 20

Start 100% applications → 80/20 → 60/20/20 → 40/20/20… Order/amounts matter less than staying flexible.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Core Four + rule of 20

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Four ways to land an offer; stop being stuck on mass apply.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Job-search channel strategy.
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> Think of your job search as a two by two grid. On one side, you have people who know you and people who don't. And on the other, you can pitch yourself to one person at a time or many at the same time. That gives you four boxes or what I call the core four of your job search.

> A true referral doesn't come from an employee filling out a form for you. It happens when that employee tells the hiring manager, I know this person and you should interview them because of XYZ.

> Just follow my rule of 20. Every 20 days that you aren't getting the results that you want, move 20% of your effort away from job applications and into one of the other boxes. Start 100% on applications, then 80-20, then 60-20-20, and then you guessed it, 40-20-20.

> So stop playing in the hardest box. Rebalance your effort and watch new doors open that you didn't even know existed.
