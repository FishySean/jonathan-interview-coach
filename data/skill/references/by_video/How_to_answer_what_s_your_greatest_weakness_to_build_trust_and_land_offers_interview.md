## Core principles

- **They don’t really care about your weakness — they’re looking for one specific strength:** proving you’ve seen a blind spot and acted on it (**growth mindset**).
- Most people are “allergic” to weaknesses — avoid, defend, blame others instead of changing.
- **Three steps:** name a **non-critical** weakness → reflect on impact → share improvement actions + results you’re seeing.
- Close: **show flaws and prove you’re stronger because of them.**

## Interview philosophy

“What’s your greatest weakness?” is a trust / growth-mindset probe. Naming a real (non-role-killing) gap plus measurable improvement beats fake strengths-as-weaknesses or denial.

## Evaluation criteria

What interviewers “see” when done right:

- Blind-spot awareness
- Courage/effort to change
- Concrete improvement system
- Early results / evidence

Avoid critical-to-the-role weaknesses.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Hiding, defending, or blaming others
- Pretending everything’s fine
- Missing that the question is really about growth strength
- Naming a weakness that is critical for the job (he specifies **non-critical**)

## Follow-up questions

- “Is this weakness non-critical for the role?”
- “What’s the impact if I don’t fix it?”
- “What specific steps am I taking — and what results so far?”

## Answer frameworks

### Three steps

1. Name a non-critical weakness
2. Reflect on its impact
3. Share what you’re doing to improve + results

Formula echo: weakness → why it matters → steps → results.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Risk-planning weakness (full sample)

- **Central question this Short addresses:** How to answer “What’s your greatest weakness?” to build trust and land offers.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** “What’s your greatest weakness?”
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> when I kick off projects, I sometimes neglect to plan for risks that could derail the project. I recognize that this is a weakness because risk management is critical for consistent and on target delivery. So I've taken steps to improve. For recent projects, I've been creating a risk register as part of every new project plan. As a result, my last three projects have planned for 100% of deviations from scope.
