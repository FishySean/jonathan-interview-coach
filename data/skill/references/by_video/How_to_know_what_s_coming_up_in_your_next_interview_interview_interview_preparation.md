## Core principles

- **Interviews are like exams** — you can exhaust yourself preparing for everything, or get the Q&A focus ahead of time.
- **Recruiters are allies, not calendar bots** — they want you to succeed because filling roles is their job.
- **Ask the right questions after “you’re advancing”** — don’t just reply “got it.”
- **Insider focus beats guessing** (e.g. stats / technical round when you were prepping behavioral).
- **Nothing guaranteed** — HR might not reply or give fluff — but you miss 100% of shots you don’t take.
- **“Is it cheating? Maybe. Is it working? Absolutely.”**

## Interview philosophy

Advancement emails are a moment to convert the recruiter into a prep partner. Vague “next steps” hide what the team doubts about you; polite, specific questions can surface that (e.g. surprise technical round because of skill uncertainty).

## Evaluation criteria

What good recruiter intel looks like:

- Feedback from previous round to carry forward.
- Main focus of next round.
- Skills/examples to highlight.
- Example win: tip that next round = statistics / technical → candidate re-preps and lands offer.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Treating recruiters like calendar bots.
- Replying only “got it / looking forward to it” to advancement emails.
- Guessing the system instead of asking how it works.
- Preparing the wrong interview type (behavioral vs technical) with no heads-up.

## Follow-up questions

Send to recruiter (see section 8). Self-check:

- “Did I ask for previous-round feedback and next-round focus?”
- “Did I ask which skills/examples to highlight?”
- “Am I still guessing, or did I ask?”

## Answer frameworks

### Advancement-email SOP

1. Thanks for the news.
2. State intent: prepare in the most relevant way.
3. Ask two questions: prior-round feedback; main focus + skills/examples to highlight.
4. Close looking forward.
