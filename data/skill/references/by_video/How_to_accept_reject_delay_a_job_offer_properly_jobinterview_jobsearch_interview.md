## Core principles

- **Got the offer — don’t blow the reply.** Four situations: negotiate, delay, reject, accept.
- **Not negotiating can be a life-sized financial mistake** — leave thousands on the table; live with could-have regret.
- Negotiation reply: **experience + market data + confidence** in one email naming your number (candidates earned $10k+).
- **Delay:** Don’t make the company that wants you wait longer — make the undecided company hurry using offer leverage + social proof.
- **Reject:** Don’t ghost; thank + clear reason + leave door open (world is small; they often come back).
- **Accept:** First impression for the new role — gratitude + confirm yes + excitement; short, confident, future-focused.
- **Don’t just sign the offer — sign the career you’re building.**

## Interview philosophy

Offer-stage communication is reputation management and leverage. How you say yes/no/wait/ask-more sets negotiation outcomes, pipeline speed, and onboarding tone.

## Evaluation criteria

- Negotiate with named number + data/confidence
- Delay via speeding up preferred company, not stalling the sure thing
- Reject with grace and open door
- Accept with gratitude, clarity, excitement

## Common candidate mistakes

- Accepting without negotiating
- Waiting the wrong way and losing both offers
- Ghosting or careless rejection emails
- Sloppy accept that sets a bad onboarding tone

## Follow-up questions

- “Did I name a number with market data and confidence?”
- “Am I hurrying my top choice with offer leverage — or making the sure offer wait?”
- “Does my no thank them, give a reason, and leave the door open?”
- “Is my yes three lines: gratitude, confirm, excitement?”

## Answer frameworks

### Four offer replies

1. **Negotiate** — one email: experience + market data + confidence → name the number  
2. **Delay / multi-offer** — tell undecided company you have an offer (speed + social proof)  
3. **Reject** — thank + clear reason + leave door open  
4. **Accept** — gratitude + confirm yes + show excitement  

## Good vs bad examples

### Negotiation

**Bad:** Just accept; leave thousands on the table.

**Good framing (verbatim):**

> use your experience and market data and confidence in one email to name your number. This short email has earned many of my candidates over $10,000 more.

### Delay

**Bad:** Make the company that wants you wait while you chase another.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Don't make the company that actually wants you to wait longer. Make the company that doesn't want you yet to hurry up. So use your existing offer leverage. Tell the undecided company that I have an offer. If they like you, they will speed up… you also get to increase your value instantly through social proof

### Reject

**Good (verbatim):**

> provide a graceful rejection, thank them, give them a clear reason and then leave the door open… This is how you say no without ending the relationship.

### Accept

**Good (verbatim):**

> lead with gratitude and confirm your yes and show excitement. Short, confident, future focused. Here's a three line reply that nails it.
