## Core principles

- **A powerful statement ends down, not up** (falling terminal pitch).
- Upward inflection sounds like asking permission — kills recommendations.
- Dropping pitch makes statements sound complete/final → get to “okay.”
- Chinese professionals often stereotyped as passive/agreeable — fix starts with voice.

## Interview philosophy

Authority is partly acoustic. Same content with falling vs rising pitch changes whether leaders trust the recommendation.

## Evaluation criteria

- Terminal pitch falls on recommendations and commitments
- Sounds like statement, not question
- Confident close without permission-seeking tone

## Common candidate mistakes

- Rising pitch on “I recommend…”
- “We can deliver on Friday?” energy → invites doubt
- Passive delivery reinforcing “easy to push over” stereotype

## Follow-up questions

- “Did my recommendation end down or up?”
- “Would a VP hear this as permission-asking or deciding?”

## Answer frameworks

**Rule:** Statement content + **falling pitch** on the final words.

## Good vs bad examples

### Budget recommendation (P&G)

**Bad (verbatim scene):**

> I recommend that we increase our brand budget, and my voice went up like I was asking for permission. Spoiler, I didn't get more budget… my manager… you had the right idea John, but the wrong voice… a powerful statement ends down, not up.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Like I recommend that we increase our brand budget.

### Friday delivery

**Bad rising → doubt (verbatim):**

> we can deliver on Friday, now they're like wait I'm not so sure about that, tell me everything.

**Good falling (verbatim):**

> But when you drop your pitch, we can deliver on Friday. It sounds like a complete statement, it sounds final, and that's how you get to okay.
