## Core principles

- **"If only this were in Chinese they'd see how smart I am" is solvable** — sounding smart is about **structure, not English**.
- **SCQA** (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) — McKinsey-style executive communication framework.
- Especially useful for **concise answers**: follow-ups after behavioral stories, knowledge/skills questions.
- **Stop answering to sound good — start answering to get hired.**

## Interview philosophy

ESL candidates often equate fluency with intelligence. Interviewers hear junior/unsure vs experienced/trustworthy based on structure. Consultants sound smart with little content because of frameworks — SCQA is that lever for candidates.

## Evaluation criteria

Score SCQA answers on:

1. **Situation** — clear context / principle
2. **Complication** — the real tension or risk
3. **Question** — the decision you faced (explicit tradeoff)
4. **Answer** — what you chose + measurable consequence

Red flags: vague "we try to be careful," process-dumping, deferring all judgment to the manager.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Believing English is the only barrier to sounding smart.
- Rambling follow-ups without a decision or tradeoff.
- Flagging risks upward without ownership ("see what manager thought").
- Trying to "sound good" instead of hiring-decision clarity.

## Follow-up questions

When answers are mushy, ask:

- "What's the Situation / Complication / Question / Answer in one sentence each?"
- "What tradeoff were you choosing between?"
- "What did *you* decide — and what did it cost/prevent?"

## Answer frameworks

**SCQA:**
- **S**ituation
- **C**omplication
- **Q**uestion (the dilemma)
- **A**nswer (decision + result)

Use for: post-story follow-ups; skills/knowledge questions.

## Good vs bad examples

### Follow-up: How did you make sure you were managing risks?

**Bad — rambling (verbatim):**

> So for risk management, I think we just try to be careful. Whenever something came up, we would just talk about it as a team. I usually follow a process that we already had, and if there were any risks, I would flag them to my manager and see what they thought.

**Jonathan's check (verbatim):**

> If you were the interviewer, would you feel good hearing that?

**Good — SCQA (verbatim):**

> I made sure risk was a consideration from the very start, because an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure. Our biggest identified risk was misalignment between product and legal. So the question for me was, do we move fast and hope the issues don't surface, or slow down to validate assumptions? I chose to pause the launch for a legal review, even though it wasn't requested. It delayed us one week, but it prevented a compliance issue that could have cost us a full quarter.

**Why it matters (verbatim):**

> Do you hear the difference there? The first sounds junior and unsure, the second, with SCQA, sounds experienced and trustworthy.
