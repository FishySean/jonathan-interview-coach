## Core principles

- Confidence has nothing to do with how you **feel** and everything to do with **who you choose to be**.
- Confidence is not where you begin — it’s where you **end**.
- Affirmations / “fake it till you make it” fade; **identity** lasts — specifically identifying as someone who **acts**.
- **Evidence over emotions.**
- Humans are hardwired for fear, not confidence; silence it by making the pain of **inaction** greater than the pain of **action**.
- “I’m going to do it scared anyway” → identity of someone who acts regardless of feeling; forgive yourself for fear.
- You lack confidence because **expectations are too high** / fear of not succeeding — but success is **inevitable** when you do the small things right; starts with **doing something**.

## Interview philosophy

(Inner game for interviews/career.) Feeling terrified while leading teams, building business, speaking on TV is compatible with acting. Interview confidence comes from reps and identity-as-actor, not mirror affirmations before the call.

## Evaluation criteria

Self-check:

- Acting despite fear vs waiting to “feel confident”
- Identity statements tied to behavior (healthy people do healthy things)
- Inaction more painful than action
- Small consistent evidence stacking

## Common candidate mistakes

- Waiting for confidence before acting.
- Affirmations in the mirror that fade in a minute.
- Faking it till you make it without identity change (Jonathan: did nothing in his 20s).
- Letting fear-of-looking-stupid block practice (gym / interview).
- Expecting big success as a feeling-goal instead of small-things-right inevitability.

## Follow-up questions

Coach:

- “Are you waiting to feel confident, or choosing who to be?”
- “Does staying the same hurt more than acting yet?”
- “What evidence are you collecting this week?”

## Answer frameworks

1. Drop affirmation-as-start; build **identity of someone who acts**.
2. Raise pain of status quo above pain of action.
3. Do the scared thing anyway → stack evidence meal-by-meal / rep-by-rep / mock-by-mock.
4. Lower false expectations; trust small right actions compound.

## Good vs bad examples

### Affirmations vs identity

**Bad:** “I am confident” in the mirror → works a minute, then fades; fake-it in 20s did nothing.

**Good:** Identify as someone who acts; **evidence over emotions**.

### Fitness identity shift (Jonathan)

**Before:** McDonald’s at 2am overtime — fine because he didn’t care about being fat; thought he was “athletic.”

**Break:** Hurt back carrying niece → identity shattered → pain great enough to change.

**After:** Stopped worrying about looking stupid in the gym; did what healthy people do → unhealthy/not-gym became more painful than the workout; bigger fears threatened the new identity.

**Line that shifts:**

> I'm going to do it scared anyway

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Core thesis

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Build true confidence from within (identity + evidence, not feelings).
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Confidence / nerves coaching transferable to interviews.
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> What if I told you that confidence has nothing to do with how you feel and everything to do with who you choose to be?

> Most people have this backwards. Confidence is not where you begin, it's where you end.

> That's why in my 20s, telling myself lies in the mirror and faking it until I made it did nothing. Because affirmations and emotions don't last. Identity does. Specifically when you identify as someone who acts. And that's why I always say, evidence over emotions.

> All you have to do is make the pain of an action greater than the pain of action. It has to hurt more to stay the same than it does to act. Make that part of your identity.

*(Note: transcript says “pain of an action greater than the pain of action” — context makes clear he means pain of **inaction** > pain of action.)*

> So when you tell yourself, I'm going to do it scared anyway, something shifts. You step into the identity of someone who takes action no matter what they feel. And you forgive yourself for your fear and uncertainty.

> See, you lack confidence because your expectations are too high. You're afraid that you won't succeed. But you don't realize success is not a goal that you set. Success is an inevitability when you do the small things right. And that starts with doing something.
