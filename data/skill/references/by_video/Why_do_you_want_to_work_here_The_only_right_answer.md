## Core principles

- **They're hiring because they're in pain** — they have a problem that needs to be solved, and they want to hear how *you* can solve it.
- **Not paying you to learn** — self-centered “I want to grow” reasons miss the point.
- **Not building a cult** — vague “I like your culture” reasons also miss the point.
- **Structure beats polish** — the less confident candidate who named the pain, proved similar work, and tied fit to Z got the job over the polished big-tech candidate.

## Interview philosophy

Hiring managers compare candidates on whether the “why us” answer reduces risk: will this person actually solve *our* operational pain, or leave when the role stops feeling like a “challenge”? Research (coffee chats + JD) that surfaces the real pain point signals seriousness; a concrete past example proves capability; a personal Z (growth stage, culture fit, product passion) makes the answer authentic without being self-centered.

## Evaluation criteria

- Does the candidate name the company’s **pain / problem** (X), not only their own growth goals?
- Do they **prove** with one concrete similar example (Y), not just claim skills?
- Do they explain why they’ll **succeed here** (Z) — culture, stage, product/customers — in a way that feels personal and authentic?
- Structure matters more than delivery confidence: Jonathan hired the weaker speaker with the stronger structure.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Self-centered reasons: “I want to grow,” “I’m looking for the next challenge.”
- Vague culture praise: “I like your culture.”
- Polished but empty answers that leave the HM thinking: why leave big tech for us? What if you’re bored in a year and waste our investment?
- Telling instead of proving — no concrete example of similar problem-solving.

## Follow-up questions

Questions Jonathan’s internal monologue asked after a weak “why us” answer (use these when coaching):

- “Why go from one tech company to another?”
- “What if a year from now this role no longer challenges you?”
- “Will you leave and make my investment in you a complete waste?”
- Candidate self-check he models: “Am I on the right track here?” after restating the pain.

## Answer frameworks

**Formula:** I’m excited about X because I can do Y, and I’m going to succeed because of Z.

Expanded script structure:

1. **X — pain:** “I’m really excited about this role because according to my understanding, you’re looking for X.” (X = pain point / problem from research)
2. **Y — proof:** “Based on that, I think my experience with Y could be helpful.” + one concrete similar example so they *envision* your skills — “you aren’t just telling them you can do it, you are proving it.”
3. **Z — why you’ll succeed here:** “I believe my impact will be multiplied here because of Z.” (cultural fit, growth stage, passion for product/customers — what makes it personal and authentic)

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Operations manager hire — “Why do you want to work here?”

**Bad (polished big-tech candidate):**

> I'm looking for the next challenge in my career. I want to grow at a dynamic, fast-moving company like yours.

**Why it matters (Jonathan’s read):** Fair / common, but triggers risk questions — why switch tech companies; will you leave when challenged less; is my investment wasted?

**Good (second candidate — coffee chat + JD + proof + fit):**

> I've had a coffee chat with Brianna on your team, and she told me, you're looking for someone who can build systems and automate and scale operations. When I combine that knowledge with what I see in your job description, it sounds like you want someone who can take initiative and solve operational bottlenecks one after the other. Am I on the right track here?

Then after confirmation:

> that's the reason I want to work here because your company is growing like a rocket ship, and I think my ability to solve problems from zero to one can help you to scale faster. For me, it's the perfect environment to use my strengths.

She then gave a short example of solving a similar problem at her current company. Jonathan: “Honestly, it wasn’t the best example, but the structure was what really got me.” She got the job.
