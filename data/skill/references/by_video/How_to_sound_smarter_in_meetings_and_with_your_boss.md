## Core principles

- Don’t choose between quiet/overlooked and **smooth talker** (fake smile, big words, no delivery).
- Win as a **smart talker**: good at communicating **and** respected.
- Smart talkers build trust on two foundations: **mutual respect** + **mutual purpose** (relationship + results).
- Fastest respect loss isn’t always a big mistake — it’s when people think you’re **disrespecting them**.
- Be the smart talker who makes others feel important — not the smooth talker who sounds important.

## Interview philosophy

(Meetings / boss / peers.) Disagreements are rarely about the goal — usually about **how** to achieve it. Tools: **contrasting** (don’t mean / do mean) and climbing one level higher to invent a shared purpose.

## Evaluation criteria

- Contrasting used when challenging ideas (protects worth + clarifies intent)
- Mutual purpose found via “why important?” + higher shared goal
- Respect preserved while driving results

## Common candidate mistakes

- Being the smooth talker (big words, no delivery).
- Staying quiet and overlooked as the only alternative.
- Hearing a challenge as an attack on self-worth (“is this idea really going to make sense to our VP?”).
- Arguing the slide/method without finding shared purpose.

## Follow-up questions

Smart-talker prompts:

- “Why is this important to you?”
- Reflect: “I'm hearing from you that you really want the VP to be confident in our numbers. Is that right?”

## Answer frameworks

### Contrasting (mutual respect)

1. Say what you **don’t** mean (remove misunderstanding).
2. Say what you **do** mean (clarify intent + build respect).

### Mutual purpose

1. Find purpose behind their idea (“Why is this important to you?”).
2. Invent mutual purpose — climb one level higher until you share a larger goal.

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Fight over cutting a financial model (Tom the smart talker)

Jonathan snaps aggressively; Tom:

> Hey, I'm not saying that your work doesn't matter. It's brilliant and I rely on it. I just think if we overload them, they'll stop listening before they even see how good it is.

**Why it matters:** Respect + restored objective; defensiveness melts.

### Scenario: Coworker challenges idea in a meeting

**Bad framing of challenge:** “is this idea really going to make sense to our VP” → you defend your worth.

**Good contrasting rewrite:**

> I don't mean to say your ideas and good your ideas are valuable. I just want to make sure that we present it in a way that lands as clearly as it deserves.

### Scenario: Disagree on how to present a slide

**Good mutual-purpose script:**

> I'm hearing from you that you really want the VP to be confident in our numbers. Is that right? Okay, well, we both want the VP to align on a recommendation. Let's find a way to make both the numbers and the story work together.
