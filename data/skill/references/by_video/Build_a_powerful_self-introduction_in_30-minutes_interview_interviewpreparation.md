## Core principles

- **You can build a strong self-intro in ~30 minutes** with a clear SOP (JD → competencies → tagline → HAS structure).
- **Use AI to extract the three most critical competencies from the JD**, then connect your experience via a customized candidate tagline.
- **HAS method:** Hook → Achievement → So what? (tie back to the role).
- **Hook uses title + years + tagline** that peaks curiosity and maps skills to core requirements.
- **Achievement is a teaser win**, not a lengthy story — spark curiosity and position as top candidate.
- **So what?** shows you understand/care about their needs and that you’re the ideal fit.

## Interview philosophy

Interviewers hear dozens of intros. A memorable tagline plus one supporting win, closed with role-specific motivation, beats a resume recitation. Customization to the JD’s critical competencies is what makes the same candidate pass Meta, ByteDance, and an AI startup in one day (his coached example).

## Evaluation criteria

Signals Jonathan optimizes for in a self-intro:

- **JD alignment:** Tagline connects skills to the company’s core requirements.
- **Curiosity / memorability:** One-liner that “peaks their curiosity” (e.g. “user whisperer”).
- **Proof teaser:** One major win with enough specificity (method + metric) to support the tagline.
- **Role care:** Explicit “so what” that ties excitement to *their* product/mission, not generic flattery.

## Common candidate mistakes

Implied by what he replaces:

- Reading the JD line-by-line slowly instead of extracting critical competencies efficiently (with AI + prompt).
- Lengthy stories in the intro instead of a teaser achievement.
- Intro that doesn’t tie back to the role’s needs (“so what” missing).
- Resume-only framing with no memorable tagline or curiosity hook.

## Follow-up questions

Prep / coach checks:

- “What are the three most critical competencies in this JD?”
- “What’s my customized candidate tagline for the competency I chose?”
- “Does my hook include title, years, and that tagline?”
- “Is my achievement a short teaser with a clear win — not a full STAR dump?”
- “Does my closing ‘so what’ show I understand *their* needs?”

## Answer frameworks

### JD teardown prompt (AI-assisted)

> We'll collect the three most critical competencies from this JD, then brainstorm ways that you can connect your experience to a chosen competency through a customized candidate tagline.

### HAS method

1. **Hook** — job title, years, and chosen candidate tagline (one-liner that peaks curiosity; connects skills to core requirements memorably).
2. **Achievement** — one major win supporting the tagline; no lengthy story; teaser only.
3. **So what?** — tie back to the role; show you understand/care about their needs and you’re the ideal fit.

## Good vs bad examples

### Efficient JD prep vs line-by-line grind

**Scenario:** Candidate has ~30 minutes and multiple interviews same day (Meta, ByteDance, AI startup).

**Bad / outdated approach (paraphrase of his contrast):** Read the JD line by line like “granny’s economy.”

**Good (verbatim):**

> So just throw it to your favorite AI with this prompt. We'll collect the three most critical competencies from this JD, then brainstorm ways that you can connect your experience to a chosen competency through a customized candidate tagline.

---

### HAS structure teaching

**Scenario:** Building the spoken self-intro after choosing a tagline.

**Hook (verbatim):**

> First comes the hook. You're going to start with your job title, years, and your chosen candidate tagline, which is the one liner that peaks their curiosity. It's going to connect your skills to their core requirements in a memorable way.

**Achievement (verbatim):**

> Next is the achievement. Drop one major win from your career that supports that tagline. No lengthy story, just a teaser to spark their curiosity and position yourself as the top candidate.

**So what (verbatim):**

> And finally, so what? Tie it back to the role. Show them that you understand and care about their needs and that you're the ideal fit.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Meta self-intro (coached candidate — full script)

- **Central question this Short addresses:** If you had 30 minutes to prepare a self-intro, how would you do it?
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** “Tell me about yourself” / opening self-introduction (Meta example).
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> As you'll see on my resume, I'm a product analyst with five years of experience in consumer tech. But one thing that my resume won't tell you is that my teams call me their user whisperer. I've excelled at the intersection of analytics and user empathy to drive sticky and impactful products. Most recently, I worked on Feature X for our flagship product. By combining user data-driven insights with qualitative user interview notes, I identified a trust gap in the product experience. I recommended and led a mid-sprint pivot, which boosted our attachment by 22%. That's why I'm so excited about Meta's AI suite, not just because you're investing in world-class talent and infrastructure, but because it's being designed to help people and understand them. I want to help build products and understand us, and I think that this is the place to do it.

- **Notes:** Placeholder `Feature X` kept as in transcript; company-specific “so what” is Meta’s AI suite.
