## Core principles

- **You and your manager define success differently.** Employees think success = deliver results + be responsible. Managers' success is managing uncertainty: shifting director priorities, team KPIs, competing for resources.
- **Managers are often more anxious than you** — including about *their* job security. Your job is to **remove uncertainties, not add to them**.
- **Deliver peace of mind, not just the end product.** Work updates should feel like Amazon tracking notifications.
- **You work in the service industry** — you work in the service of your boss, regardless of function.
- **Proactive bad news builds trust.** Delay + clarity + next step beats silence.

## Interview philosophy

Jonathan speaks as a manager who overnight redefined success after promotion. Recognition and reward flow from being the person who **reduces managerial anxiety** — transparent status, no surprises, collaborative course-correction.

In interviews / workplace storytelling, this maps to: how you escalate risks, how you update stakeholders, and whether a hiring manager can trust you won't hide bad news.

## Evaluation criteria

Score whether the person:

1. **Reduces uncertainty** — Clear ETA, status, blockers, and recovery plan
2. **Surfaces problems early** — Doesn't wait until failure is irreversible
3. **Treats updates as tracking** — In-progress visibility, not only final delivery
4. **Invites partnership** — Manager can redirect priorities because they were informed in time

Red flags: "it'll arrive soon" then silence; hiding delays; surprising the manager at the last minute.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Equating "doing the job well" with what managers actually need (certainty).
- Only communicating when work is finished.
- Staying silent when off track (hoping to catch up before anyone notices).
- Adding to the manager's uncertainty instead of removing it.

## Follow-up questions

When stories about manager relationships are weak, ask:

- "When something slipped, how did you update your manager — and when?"
- "Walk me through a status update you sent that wasn't all good news."
- "What uncertainty were you removing for your manager in that project?"
- "If Amazon-style tracking applied to your work, what notifications would your manager have gotten?"

## Answer frameworks

**Amazon-delivery metaphor for work:**
1. Promise a clear delivery window (not "soon")
2. Send progress tracking (left warehouse / in transit / out for delivery)
3. Provide proof of delivery
4. Goal: peace of mind, not just the toboggan (deliverable)

**Off-track update template (Jonathan's words):**
> Hey, quick update. I expect to deliver the report three days later than we discussed. I ran into this issue with data latency, but I've aligned on the issue with the data team and they're going to ship the fix tonight, just letting you know.

**Reframe:** Job with manager = reduce their uncertainty + deliver a great experience (not merely "deliver work").

## Good vs bad examples

### Employee success vs manager success

**Scenario:** Defining what "doing well" means with your boss.

**Employee view (Jonathan, verbatim):**

> As employees, we think success means doing the job well, right? Like delivering results, being responsible. All of this is true.

**Manager view after promotion (verbatim):**

> But after I became a manager, what did change overnight was my definition for success. Because now I had to deal with changing priorities for my director, pressure to deliver on our team's KPIs. There were other managers who competed with me for resources. You need to deal with a lot more uncertainty.

**Why it matters (verbatim):**

> And that's why managers are always anxious. They might be more anxious than you are about your job security. And your job is to help them remove uncertainties, not add to it.

---

### Amazon toboggan: silence vs tracking

**Scenario:** Communicating progress on deliverables.

**Jonathan's setup (verbatim):**

> Recently, it's been crazy snowy here in Canada, so I bought a toboggan on Amazon. Now, my wife is from Shanghai, so she's never had any real snow growing up. And I really wanted to take her tobogganing for the first time, just like I did as a CBC kid growing up here in Canada.

> But here's the thing. The weather's changing fast. We both have really busy schedules. And in the back of our minds, we're thinking, what if the snow melts before this arrives, right?

**Bad — Amazon with silence (verbatim):**

> Now imagine if Amazon just said, your toboggan will arrive soon, and then silence. I'd be checking the window every day, refreshing my app, stressed, right? You know what? Maybe we shouldn't even buy this toboggan.

**Good — real Amazon experience (verbatim):**

> But what is the actual Amazon experience? Well, Amazon tells me your order will arrive in three days. Hey, it left the warehouse yesterday. Now it's in transit. Now it's out for delivery. They even take a photo of the drop off and they send me proof.

**Punchline (verbatim):**

> Do you feel that difference? Amazon's not just delivering a toboggan. They're delivering peace of mind.

> And you too can think of your work as a delivery. Not just the end product of your work, but the peace of mind for your manager.

> No matter what work you do, you actually work in the service industry because you work in the service of your boss.

---

### Off-track update → trust (not blame)

**Scenario:** Work is delayed.

**Bad:** Hide the delay; hope to catch up; surprise the manager.

**Good — update as tracking notification (verbatim):**

> Even if your work is off track, your manager wants to know, hey, quick update. I expect to deliver the report three days later than we discussed. I ran into this issue with data latency, but I've aligned on the issue with the data team and they're going to ship the fix tonight, just letting you know.

**Manager's internal reaction (verbatim):**

> And you know what? If I'm the manager, I know I'd look right past the delay, as frustrating as it is. And I think to myself, I can trust you. You won't hide things from me. You won't surprise me. You will work with me to ensure that we're on track.

**Bonus: informed managers can unblock you (verbatim):**

> And because there is so much uncertainty in my work, maybe I know that priorities have shifted and I can give you some feedback. Like, oh yeah, don't worry about that data point. The VP doesn't care anymore about that. Let's just ship it now and get it out.
