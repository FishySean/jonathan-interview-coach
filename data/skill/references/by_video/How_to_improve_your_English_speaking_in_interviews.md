## Core principles

- **Fluent interview speaking = three separate channels:** how you sound, how you look, how you think — fix them one by one.
- **Most people over-focus on words.** Long memorized scripts fail because sound and body language never get isolated practice.
- **Best improvement method isn't another how-to video — it's watching yourself** via mock interviews.
- **Self-mock interview = three isolation passes:** audio only → video only → words only (transcript).
- **Most people never actually hear themselves** in interview mode — that's why speaking doesn't improve.
- **Bonus:** a recording is something you can send an expert for professional opinion.

## Interview philosophy

Jonathan coaches Chinese professionals with a self-mock interview method. Interview performance is multi-modal: vocal confidence, body language/eye contact, and structured thinking on the transcript. Isolating each channel reveals problems memorization hides (ums, speed-ups, fidgeting, missing impact).

## Evaluation criteria

When reviewing a mock recording, score three passes separately:

1. **Audio (sound)** — Filler (um), nervous speeding up, shaky/unconfident voice
2. **Video (look)** — Fidgeting, chair turning, aggressive frowning while thinking, eye contact with interviewer
3. **Words (think)** — Structure, logical line of thinking, easy to understand; spot rambling, vague phrasing, repeated fluff; notice missing impact and achievements

Red flags: only rewriting scripts; never listening to or watching themselves; answers that look fine on paper but sound shaky / look disconnected.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Writing long scripts and memorizing them — then wondering why it never works.
- Watching more how-to videos instead of watching their own mock interviews.
- Never hearing their own interview audio (ums, speed, shaky voice go unnoticed).
- Missing body-language cues because audio distracts (fidgeting, no eye contact, frown-while-thinking).
- Never reading their own transcript as the interviewer — missing rambling, fluff, missing impact.

## Follow-up questions

When a coachee says "my English isn't good enough":

- "Have you done a full mock on camera — and reviewed audio, video, and transcript separately?"
- "On audio-only: where do you um, speed up, or sound shaky?"
- "On video-only: fidgeting, eye contact, facial expression while thinking?"
- "On the transcript: is the answer structured? What's rambling or missing impact?"
- "Can you send this recording to someone who can give a professional opinion?"

## Answer frameworks

**Self-mock interview method — three passes:**

**Pass 1 — Audio only (how you sound):**
1. Volume all the way up
2. Flip screen down
3. Just listen
4. Notice: ums, speeding up when nervous, shaky/unconfident voice

**Pass 2 — Video only (how you look):**
1. Screen up, mute sound, press play
2. Just watch body language
3. Notice: fidgeting (e.g. necklace), turning in chair, frowning while thinking, eye contact gaps

**Pass 3 — Words only (how you think):**
1. Transcribe the mock interview
2. Read the answer as if you are the interviewer
3. Check: structured? logical and easy to understand?
4. Spot: rambling, vague phrasing, repeated fluff; missing impact and achievements

**Bonus:** Send the recording to an expert for a professional opinion.

## Good vs bad examples

### Secret vs common approach

**Scenario:** Wanting to speak more fluently in interviews.

**Jonathan's secret (verbatim):**

> The real secret to speaking fluently in interviews? It's separating how you sound, how you look, and how you think, and fixing them one by one.

**Bad — words/scripts only (verbatim):**

> See, most people are going to focus on the words. Like, they write these long scripts and memorize them, and then they wonder why it never works. Sound familiar?

**Good — watch yourself (verbatim):**

> And the best way to improve isn't to watch another how-to video. It's to watch yourself. I'm talking about mock interviews, and my self-mock interview method has helped hundreds of Chinese professionals. It covers three distinct steps where we isolate each of these elements.

---

### Pass 1 — audio only discoveries

**Scenario:** First review pass.

**Method (verbatim):**

> So the first pass is audio only. You're going to turn the volume on your phone all the way up, you're going to flip your screen down, and you're just going to listen. It's like how blind people get way better at hearing, right? You will naturally focus on how you sound.

**What people notice (verbatim):**

> Wow, do I say um so much? I never noticed. Do I always speed up when I get nervous? My voice sounds really shaky there. It sounds so unconfident.

**Why it matters (verbatim):**

> Most people never actually hear themselves in interviews, and that's why their speaking never improves.

---

### Pass 2 — video only discoveries

**Scenario:** Second review pass.

**Method (verbatim):**

> Now the second pass is video only. You're going to turn your phone back up, you're going to mute the sound, press play, and just watch. Now you're reading all the body language cues that you missed before.

**What people notice (verbatim):**

> Oh wow, I'm fidgeting so much with my necklace and turning back and forth in my chair. Do I always frown aggressively like that when I'm thinking? Oh, I didn't even make eye contact with the interviewer for a whole two minutes.

---

### Pass 3 — words only / transcript as interviewer

**Scenario:** Third review pass.

**Method (verbatim):**

> Now the third pass is words only. Get your mock interview transcribed into text, and then read your answer, and imagine you're the interviewer. Is your answer structured? Is your line of thinking logical and easy to understand? You're going to spot rambling, vague phrasing, or repeated fluff that you never realized was there. Or you may notice stuff that's missing, like impact and achievements.

**Bonus (verbatim):**

> And here's the bonus. After recording yourself, you have something to send to an expert so that they can give you a professional opinion. Maybe an expert like me. So yeah, I'm looking forward to seeing your recording.
