## Core principles

- Thank-you email isn’t just politeness chore — top candidates use it for a **second impression**.
- Right follow-up shows you **listened**, understood **priorities**, and you’re already **part of the team**.
- Framework: **Three G’s — Gratitude, Gap, Gift**.
- Prefer **simple, casual** wording over overly formal/robotic (especially ESL) — signals relationship-building.
- Average candidates send thanks; top candidates send **value**.

## Interview philosophy

Post-interview email continues the conversation: replay HM pain, connect your talent as a gift (not a hard sell), leave more excited about the role.

## Evaluation criteria

- Casual gratitude (not stiff)
- Specific gap/pain from the interview (not generic)
- Gift = idea + proof of relevant impact + enthusiasm
- Could only fit that conversation / company

## Common candidate mistakes

- Treating thank-you as checkbox only.
- Overly formal, robotic ESL tone.
- Thanks with no listened-pain / no value add.
- Hard-selling instead of connecting dots to their problem.

## Follow-up questions

In-interview Q that feeds the email:

- “What's the top priority you'd like the new hire to solve in the first 90 days?”

## Answer frameworks

### Three G’s

1. **Gratitude** — simple, casual thanks.
2. **Gap** — replay the pain/priority you heard (what keeps HM up at night).
3. **Gift** — how you might help + relevant evidence + genuine enthusiasm.

## Good vs bad examples

### Bad (implied)

Ignored/generic thank-you that only says thanks.

### Good — full template in action

> Hi, Sarah. Thank you again for taking the time to speak with me yesterday. I really enjoyed our conversation and learning more about the exciting work your team is doing at Bananaco. I was fascinated by your current challenge to consolidate data from different platforms. Understanding the roadblocks your analysts face, I'm excited to dig deeper into this problem. From my experience building data architecture that cut reporting time by 40% and created a single source of truth, I can see how this perspective could help with your data integration project. It's why I'm leaving this interview even more excited about the role than when I began. Looking forward to what's ahead.

**Mapping:** Gratitude (thanks/enjoyed) → Gap (consolidate data / analyst roadblocks) → Gift (40% reporting cut / single source of truth → their integration project).
