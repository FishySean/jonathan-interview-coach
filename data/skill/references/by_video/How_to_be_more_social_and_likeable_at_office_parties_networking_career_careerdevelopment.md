## Core principles

- You’re not invisible because they don’t care — **you’re invisible because you’re afraid to be seen.**
- Quiet nodding / work-only version never gets let in.
- **Three steps:** (1) simple observation icebreaker → (2) deeper casual topic + give a piece of yourself → (3) graceful exit, then **circle back** for a high-note goodbye.
- Don’t need charisma or perfect English — need an eye for details + willingness to say “I see you.”
- **You’re choosing the final note they’ll remember you by.**

## Interview philosophy

Workplace likability is approachable initiation + reciprocal self-disclosure + closure. Same skills help networking and “culture fit” presence beyond pure interview Q&A.

## Evaluation criteria

- Observation opener (drink, music, watch…)
- Deeper question + self-share
- Exit without making that the last memory — circle back pleasure-chatting line

## Common candidate mistakes

- Corner-laughing, waiting to be noticed, sipping into disappearance
- Showing only quiet work-only self
- Leaving after “I’m getting a refill” with no circle-back

## Follow-up questions

- “What detail can I observe to open?”
- “What deeper question + piece of myself can I offer?”
- “Before I leave, whom do I circle back to?”

## Answer frameworks

### Three steps

1. **Observation** — e.g. interesting drink → what is it?  
2. **Deeper + self** — how they joined; big quarter energy; best office party (I can start)  
3. **Exit + circle back** — refill/see you later → later: heading out, pleasure chatting  

## Good vs bad examples

### Invisible corner vs three-step flip

**Bad scene (verbatim):**

> At every office party, there's always one person in the corner pretending to laugh so hard that their cheeks hurt. That's you… you nod and you smile and you sip your drink and you slowly disappear.

**Truth (verbatim):**

> You're not invisible because they don't care. You're invisible because you're afraid to be seen. If all they ever see is the quiet, nodding, work-only version of you, then they will never really let you in.

### Step 1 observation (verbatim)

> that's an interesting drink you have. What is it? They grin… It's a blueberry mojito. How about your drink? You don't need charisma… You just need an eye for the interesting details… and you need the willingness to make a simple observation that says, I see you.

### Step 2 deeper (verbatim)

> what's the story behind you joining this team? How did you end up at this company? Or big celebration for a big quarter yesterday, I was so exhausted at the end, how was it for you? Or I'm curious, what's the best office party that you've ever been to? I can start.

### Step 3 close (verbatim)

> I'm going to go get a refill. See you later… Before you leave the event, take a minute and circle back… I'm heading out, but it was such a pleasure chatting with you. This one thoughtful gesture brings closure and ends your chat on a high note.

**Close (verbatim):**

> Take one step out, offer one observation, one detail about yourself, and you will see the room's been waiting for you too.
