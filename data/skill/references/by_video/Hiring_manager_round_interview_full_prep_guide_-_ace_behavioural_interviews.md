## Core principles

- **Second-round prep is a timed SOP** (same spirit as his first-round SOP): competencies → stories → story bank → practice.
- **Predict competencies, not exact questions** — skills the hiring manager will probe.
- **Round 1 vs Round 2:** HR asks “Are you qualified?” via common questions; the hiring manager asks “Are you the best candidate?” via behavioral questions.
- **Build a story bank** — blanking on behavioral Qs is the fastest way to lose HM trust; don’t rely on memory alone.
- **Genuine > recited** — HMs trust candidates who sound real more than perfect line-by-line scripts.

## Interview philosophy

Hiring managers care whether your competencies map to *their* pressure problems (e.g. misaligned teams → stakeholder management). They judge stories on validation (did others care?), challenge (was it hard?), and impact (did the business change?). Senior signal: a one-liner approach thesis (“Most people do X. I do Y because Z”) before the story.

## Evaluation criteria

For each project / story:

1. **Validation** — Did anyone else care? (manager praise, asked to present)
2. **Challenge** — Was it actually hard? (unclear direction, high stakes, tight timelines, resistance)
3. **Impact** — Did the business change? (time, money, effort saved)

Prep quality signals:

- Top **five core competencies** from the JD (AI-assisted OK)
- Top **three projects** after scoring all remembered projects
- **Story ↔ competency matrix** so every competency has coverage
- Per-project **approach statement**: Most people do X. I do Y because Z.
- Practice loop until confident — **without** memorizing wording

## Common candidate mistakes

- Trying to guess exact questions instead of competencies.
- Treating round 2 like round 1 (generic Qs) instead of behavioral “best candidate” proof.
- Filtering stories too early — don’t filter until you’ve listed every meaningful/recent project.
- Blanking because stories aren’t banked / detailed enough.
- Memorizing scripts for perfect grammar — sounds recited, loses trust.
- Under-detailing stories (STAR/CAR/HEALER all fine — “no amount of detail is too much” in the bank phase).

## Follow-up questions

Self-check while prep’ing (as if you were the HM):

- “If I were the hiring manager, why would these competencies matter to me?”
- “What problems am I under pressure to solve?”
- “How would these skills make my life easier?”
- Per project: “Did anyone else care?” / “Was this actually hard?” / “Did the business change?”

## Answer frameworks

### Timed SOP (hiring-manager / second round)

| Block | Time | Goal |
|-------|------|------|
| 1 | 20 min | Identify top 5 competencies from JD (AI OK); ask why they matter to the HM |
| 2 | 40 min | Dump all meaningful projects; score Validation / Challenge / Impact; pick top 3 |
| 3 | 90 min | Build story bank + competency matrix; over-detail; write “Most people X / I Y because Z” per project |
| 4 | 30 min | Practice stories with a simple loop — genuine delivery, not memorization |

Frameworks allowed for writing details: **STAR, CAR, or HEALER** (“MyHealer” in transcript) — choose any; depth matters more than label.

### Approach thesis (before the story)

> Most people do X. I do Y because Z.

Example:

> Most people handle conflict by compromising, but I try to understand the perspective first because I believe that true collaboration happens when I meet people where they are.

Then: “Here’s an example” → share the story.

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Standing out on behavioral / conflict-related stories

**Weak pattern (implied):** Rely on memory, blank in the room, or recite a memorized script word-for-word.

**Good — approach statement + story:**

> Most people handle conflict by compromising, but I try to understand the perspective first because I believe that true collaboration happens when I meet people where they are. Here's an example and then you would share your story.

**Why it matters:** “This instantly makes you come across as more senior and easier to trust.”

### Scenario: Round framing

**Round 1 (HR):** “Are you qualified?” → common interview questions.

**Round 2 (hiring manager):** “Are you the best candidate?” → behavioral questions.

### Scenario: Why stakeholder management shows up

> Maybe this role works across multiple teams who aren't always aligned, and that is why stakeholder management suddenly becomes critical.
