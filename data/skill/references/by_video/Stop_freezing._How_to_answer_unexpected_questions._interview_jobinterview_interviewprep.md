## Core principles

- Frozen on an unexpected question? Save it by spotting the **skill they’re really testing** behind the surface ask.
- Magic reframe: “Have you used Power BI?” → “Can you communicate data visually so people get it?” → answer becomes yes (via core skill).
- **Three-step rescue SOP:** (1) Admit you don’t know / ask for explanation, (2) Call out the core skill and confirm, (3) Connect a past story that proves excellence + invite to tell more.
- You can’t invent missing tool XP mid-interview — seize the moment to show **core competencies they really care about.**
- Next silence: freeze or **lead**?

## Interview philosophy

Unexpected tool/experience questions test honesty under pressure and ability to zoom to transferable competencies. Interviewers can teach Power BI in a week; great communicators are rare.

## Evaluation criteria

- Honesty + context-seeking (coworker-like)
- Reframe to core competency (confirm with interviewer)
- Proof story + ask to expand
- Shift picture from missing skill → you in the role

## Common candidate mistakes

- Freeze / hide gap / fake tool knowledge
- Stay stuck on narrow tool instead of core skill
- Admit gap then stop without reframe + proof

## Follow-up questions

- “What’s the core skill behind this tool/ask?”
- “Did I confirm the reframe with them?”
- “What story proves that core skill?”

## Answer frameworks

### Rescue SOP

1. **Admit + ask:** “I don’t have experience with that tool. Could you please explain it to me?”  
2. **Reframe + confirm:** “Beyond the tool… looking for someone who can X. Is that right?”  
3. **Proof + invite:** “That reminds me of… Can I tell you a bit more about that?”  

## Good vs bad examples

### Power BI freeze vs reframe

**Surface → core (verbatim):**

> You'll change a question like, have you used Power BI into, can you communicate data visually? So people get it. The answer is yes.

**Step 1 (verbatim):**

> I don't have experience with that tool. Could you please explain it to me?… You're doing what you would do in a real work setting… ask your coworker to give you more context.

**Step 2 (verbatim):**

> So beyond the tool itself, it sounds like you're really looking for a candidate who can communicate concepts visually and clearly to a non-technical audience. Is that right?… We can teach anyone Power BI in a week, but great communicators are far more rare.

**Step 3 (verbatim):**

> Like that reminds me of a project where I had to present complex data to non-technical stakeholders, which actually resulted in the largest deal in our company's history. Can I tell you a bit more about that?
