## Core principles

- Western interviewers don’t want these in a self-intro: **responsible, fast learner, hard worker**.
- Those qualities are valuable but **won’t make you stand out** — Chinese candidates overuse them vs white candidates (Jonathan’s hiring observation).
- Translate Chinese values into **results Western interviewers want to see**.
- Prefer: **I take ownership** / **I’m resourceful** / **I’m impact driven** — each with a proof story.

## Interview philosophy

They’re not paying you to learn — they’re paying you to **deliver**. Following orders is common; ownership without being told is rare. Fast learning only matters if applied to results. Hard work without leverage doesn’t get promoted.

## Evaluation criteria

- Ownership without needing orders (vs “responsible” / follow orders)
- Applied learning → results (vs “fast learner” list)
- Leverage / automation / prioritization / impact (vs “hard worker” grind)

## Common candidate mistakes

- Self-intro stuffed with responsible / fast learner / hard worker.
- Being “responsible” by doing everything yourself in group projects — missing what the team needs to succeed.
- Claiming fast learner but freezing when asked what you learned that made a difference.
- Grinding longer hours without leverage while someone else gets promoted.

## Follow-up questions

Interviewer probes Jonathan uses:

- After “fast learner”: “Okay, tell me about something you learned that made a difference.”

Coach reframes:

- “What does the team actually need to succeed?” (not only finishing my tasks)
- “How fast can you apply what you’ve learned to create results?”

## Answer frameworks

| Avoid saying | Say instead | Proof pattern |
|--------------|-------------|---------------|
| I’m responsible | I take ownership | Stepped outside role → team outcome |
| I’m a fast learner | I’m resourceful | Learned tool → delivered zero-error migration |
| I’m a hard worker | I’m impact driven | Automation / leverage → hours saved → strategy focus |

## Good vs bad examples

### Responsible → ownership

**Bad signal:** “I'm responsible” → interviewer hears “another Chinese candidate who likes to follow orders.”

**Childhood/work analogy:** Only Chinese kid does whole group project alone → still falls apart because focus was finishing tasks, not team success. Workplace = one big group project.

**Good:**

> So instead of saying, I'm responsible, say, I take ownership. When we lost a project manager, I stepped up outside of my responsibilities to lead the team and help us launch on time and on budget.

### Fast learner → resourceful

**Bad:** Claims fast learner; freezes when asked for learning that made a difference — can list learnings, not uses.

**Good:**

> So instead of saying, I'm a fast learner, say, I'm resourceful. When we switched to Salesforce, I binge watched tutorials and migrated our entire customer base with zero errors.

### Hard worker → impact driven

**Bad:** Work until 2am competing with midnight grinders; someone else promoted via leverage.

**Good:**

> So instead of saying I'm a hard worker, say I'm impact driven. During busy season, I built an automated report that saved my team 10 hours a week. So we could focus on strategy, not spreadsheets.
