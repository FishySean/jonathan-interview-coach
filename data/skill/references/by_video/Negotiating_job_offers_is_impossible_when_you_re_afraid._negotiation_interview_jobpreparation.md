## Core principles

- After many rejections, the offer moment is when you’re most likely to **grab without thinking** — biggest mistake.
- **Always negotiate what you’re worth** — offer means you’re the top candidate; top candidates deserve top compensation.
- **Scarcity trap:** fear of losing makes you forget what you deserve; believe it’s the only chance.
- **Rejection doesn’t shrink your value** — means wrong match so far; **scarcity shrinks your vision.**
- **Never let scarcity set your price.**
- Also: don’t lead intros with “I was laid off” — interviews focus on strengths.

## Interview philosophy

Negotiation fails when trauma from rejection rewrites self-worth downward. Separate “got an offer” joy from “signed instantly without asking.”

## Evaluation criteria

- Lead with strengths, not layoff in first breath
- After offer: negotiate before signing
- Price set by value/market, not scarcity fear

## Common candidate mistakes

**Candidate M:**

- Opening with layoff
- Signing immediately after “got the offer”
- Scarcity: any job after 20+ failures

## Follow-up questions

- “Am I signing from joy or from scarcity?”
- “Did I negotiate as a top candidate?”
- “Is my intro leading with strengths or layoff?”

## Answer frameworks

1. Fix storytelling (strengths first)  
2. Celebrate offer  
3. Negotiate before sign — company already chose you  
4. Reject scarcity pricing
