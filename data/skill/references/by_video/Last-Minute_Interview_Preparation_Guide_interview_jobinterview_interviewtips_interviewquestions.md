## Core principles

- Panic interview? **15-minute homework** still works: values (5) → business (5) → interviewer (5).
- Insert one resonating company value into “tell me about yourself” and “why here.”
- Explain what they do in **one sentence a five-year-old could understand**; skim news for positive developments.
- LinkedIn interviewer: career decisions + summary → meaningful end questions that create positive vibes.
- Signals: great fit, passionate, resourceful — beats 80% who only Google “questions to ask.”

## Interview philosophy

Smart short prep beats generic cramming. Specific value/business/interviewer hooks prove fit and homework.

## Evaluation criteria

- Named company value + personal proof (title + metric)
- Simple business explanation + news hook in small talk
- Interviewer-specific Q&A that smiles

## Common candidate mistakes

- Zero prep then panic
- Generic Google question lists
- No value/business/interviewer triangulation

## Follow-up questions

- “Which careers-page value do I resonate with + proof story?”
- “Can I explain their business in one kid-simple sentence?”
- “What’s one LinkedIn detail for a smile-inducing question?”

## Answer frameworks

### 15-minute triangle

1. **Values (5 min)** — pick one; weave into intro / why here  
2. **Business (5 min)** — one-sentence what they do + news nugget  
3. **Interviewer (5 min)** — LinkedIn → end questions  

## Good vs bad examples

### Value insert (verbatim)

> I noticed that customer obsession is a core value of your company. I think I'd fit your culture like a glove because in my past roles, I've been known as the customer champion due to my customer empathy. I even led a customer engagement project that increased NPS by 12%.

### Business small talk (verbatim)

> I read that you recently launched a new product. I'm sure that it must be an exciting moment for your team. I am equally thrilled at the possibility of applying my analysis skills to identify opportunities for growth and market expansion of your new product.

### Interviewer Q (verbatim)

> I saw that you've been in this company for five years. I'm curious what has kept you here and what you've enjoyed about your role.
