## Core principles

- Everyone wants the dream offer; almost no one wants to **pay what it costs**.
- Successful candidates run a **job-search system** — treat searching like a job.
- Rely on **process, not motivation**.
- **Discipline without a system is just suffering.**
- Success isn’t talent — it’s **showing up when talented people won’t**; pay the price every day.

## Interview philosophy

Offers come from intentional resume design (what recruiters actually want), daily applications through rejection, and **interview/communication skill built before invites** — mock interviews as feedback loops, not last-minute validation.

## Evaluation criteria

System markers:

- Daily application volume with a designed resume
- Skills practice before interviews arrive
- Mocks used for feedback, not ego
- Goals/schedule driven by personal conversion data (apps → interviews)

## Common candidate mistakes

- Wanting big logo/title/feeling of success without applications, story craft, or discipline.
- Waiting for motivation instead of process.
- Learning interview skills only after getting invites.
- Using mocks only as confidence theater before a real interview.
- Discipline without a tracking system → burnout/suffering.

## Follow-up questions

Coach:

- “Are you treating job search like a job today?”
- “Are you practicing interview skills before invites, or only after?”
- “Is your mock interview a feedback loop or just validation?”

## Answer frameworks

### Job-search system (what winners do)

1. Learn what recruiters want → design resume with intention.
2. Apply every day with that resume through rejection streaks.
3. Build interview + communication skills **before** invites.
4. Mock interviews = feedback loop.
5. Track data; set daily apply goals and schedule from conversion rates.

## Good vs bad examples

### Want vs cost

**Want:** Big company on resume / impressive title / feeling of success.

**Cost people avoid:** 1,000 applications & 999 rejections; time to learn career stories; daily discipline.

### Process vs motivation

**Bad:** Wait to feel motivated.

**Good:** Follow the system even when it’s not exciting or fun — “necessary.”

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Core thesis

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Simple truth of landing more offers faster — system + discipline.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Job-search strategy coaching (not one interview Q).
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> Well, as a coach, the most successful candidates that I've seen are following a job search system. They're treating job searching like a job. They're learning what recruiters actually want to see in a resume and designing it with intention. They're applying for jobs every day with that resume, even if all they see are rejection after rejection. And they're learning interview skills and building communication skills before receiving invites not afterwards. They're using mock interviews as a feedback loop, not as validation before an interview. Basically, they rely on process, not motivation.

> I know, none of this is exciting, none of it's fun, but it's necessary because discipline without a system is just called suffering.

> In the end, success is not about talent. It's about showing up when talented people won't. Because everybody wants the dream offer, but the ones who earn it, well, they pay the price every day.
