## Core principles

- **Nobody cares about your career as much as you do** — if you don’t decide what you want, your manager will.
- Always be **learning or earning**; if neither, leave.
- **9–5 = job success; 5–9 = career success.**
- Career is limited by **skills or beliefs** — never let beliefs hold you back.
- If you’re the smartest in the room, you’re in the **wrong room**.
- **Imposter syndrome** = proof you’ve leveled up, not a weakness.
- Stay **productive**, not just busy; make others feel smarter (don’t compete in the “humble Olympics” / 谦虚 — stay invisible).
- **Hard work alone isn’t enough**; if you’re irreplaceable, you’re **unpromotable**.
- You **train your manager** how to treat you; every boss teaches (even bad ones).
- Manager manages you — **own your own development**.
- Reputation: don’t write/say anything you wouldn’t put on the WSJ front page / say to their face; gossipers gossip about you too.
- **Work-life balance** isn’t given — you create it. **HR isn’t your friend.** Work isn’t your family.

## Interview philosophy

Career agency over passive loyalty. Visibility and leverage beat silent grinding. Growth is self-owned; companies optimize for the company.

## Evaluation criteria

Self-audit signals Jonathan lists: learning/earning, room smarter than you, productive vs busy, promotable (not irreplaceable), reputation hygiene, boundaries with HR/family framing.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Letting the manager decide your path.
- Staying when neither learning nor earning.
- Beliefs capping the career.
- Humble-Olympics invisibility (谦虚).
- Grinding to be irreplaceable → unpromotable.
- Expecting manager/HR/company to gift growth or work-life balance.
- Treating workplace as family.

## Follow-up questions

Coach prompts:

- “Are you learning or earning right now — or neither?”
- “Are you the smartest person in the room?”
- “Are you irreplaceable — and is that blocking promotion?”
- “How are you training your manager to treat you?”

## Answer frameworks

Rapid maxims (use as coaching reframes, not one interview script):

1. Decide what you want before the manager does.
2. Learn or earn → else leave.
3. Invest 5–9 for career, not only 9–5 for the job.
4. Replace irreplaceable grind with promotable leverage.
5. Own development; treat every boss as a teacher.

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Visibility / humility

**Bad:** Competing in the “humble Olympics” / being 谦虚 to stay invisible — “You don't get a gold medal… You just stay invisible.”

**Good direction:** Be the person who makes others feel smarter; stay productive instead of merely busy.

### Scenario: Hard work

**Bad:** Hard work alone / becoming irreplaceable.

**Good:** Don’t be unpromotable by being irreplaceable — train your manager well; own growth.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Core maxims (rapid-fire)

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Eight years of career coaching compressed to two minutes.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Broader career coaching / mindset (not one classic interview Q).
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> Nobody cares about your career as much as you do. If you don't decide what you want, your manager will decide for you. Always either be learning or earning. If you aren't doing either, you should be leaving. Your job success depends on your nine to five. Your career success depends on your five to nine. Your career will always be limited by either your skills or your beliefs. Never let your beliefs hold you back. If you're the smartest person in the room, then you're in the wrong room. Imposter syndrome is not a weakness. It's proof that you've leveled up.

> Hard work alone is not the answer. If you're irreplaceable, then you're unpromotable. You train your manager how to treat you. So train them well. Every boss is a teacher, even the bad ones. Those teach you what type of boss you don't wanna be. Your manager's job is to manage you, not to help you to grow. Own your own development.

> You don't deserve work-life balance. No company can give it to you. You have to create it for yourself. HR is not your friend. They work for the company. No matter what they say, your work is not your family. Your family is your family. Never forget that.
