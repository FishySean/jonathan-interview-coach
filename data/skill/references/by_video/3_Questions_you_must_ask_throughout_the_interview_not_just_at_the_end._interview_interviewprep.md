## Core principles

- **Don’t wait for “Do you have any questions for me?”** — 90% of candidates wait until then and are already too late.
- **Three opportunities** to ask and gather information: beginning, during, and end — works every role/level.
- **Lead from the first question** — stop waiting for permission to ask.
- Best interviews feel like **conversations** for both sides; thoughtful follow-ups beat “okay, next question.”

## Interview philosophy

Interviewers reward candidates who research the JD, ask what to focus on, clarify mid-answer, and turn end-of-interview Q&A into a real dialogue. Asking early signals “delivering solutions, not scripts.”

## Evaluation criteria

- Did the candidate ask what matters *to this interviewer* before dumping a script?
- Mid-interview: clarification and check-ins without looking lost.
- End: listen → respond thoughtfully to their answer (not a rigid next-question list).

## Common candidate mistakes

- Saving all questions for the end.
- Assuming what “tell me about yourself” should cover without asking.
- Assuming incorrectly and answering the wrong question.
- Talking too much without “Does this answer your question?”
- At Q&A: “Okay, so my next question is…” instead of engaging their answer.

## Follow-up questions

Templates candidates should use:

- Beginning: ask which area to focus on among JD competencies.
- Mid: “Just to confirm, do you mean X or Y?”
- Mid: “Does this answer your question?”
- Mid (low engagement): “Is there anything else that you'd like to hear?”
- End follow-up: resonate with their answer + bring a short proof from current role.

## Answer frameworks

### Opportunity 1 — after “Tell me about yourself”

Template:

> Happy to share. Based on what I know from the job description, you're looking for [competencies]. Before I dive in, is there a particular area that you'd like me to focus on, or should I cover all three? I want to make sure that I highlight the most relevant parts based on your needs.

### Opportunity 2 — mid-interview

- Clarify: “Just to confirm, do you mean X or Y?”
- Check-in: “Does this answer your question?”
- Prompt: “Is there anything else that you'd like to hear?”

### Opportunity 3 — end Q&A → conversation

Ask → listen → respond with resonance + personal proof + link to the role.

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Opening “Tell me about yourself”

**Bad:** Launch straight into a prepared script.

**Good (verbatim template with example competencies):**

> happy to share. Based on what I know from the job description, you're looking for exceptional stakeholder management, ambiguous problem-solving skills, and the ability to influence without authority. Before I dive in, is there a particular area that you'd like me to focus on, or should I cover all three? I want to make sure that I highlight the most relevant parts based on your needs.

**Why it matters:** Interviewer sees research + solution focus, not scripts.

### Scenario: End Q&A follow-up

**Bad:** “Okay, so my next question is…”

**Good — after asking “what's one characteristic that top performers have shared in this role” and hearing “ownership”:**

> interesting to hear that ownership is a key characteristic. I can resonate because this is also what I really value and strive to achieve. In my current role, I'm the team's go-to person for solving ambiguous problems. I'm really proud of that and excited about potentially bringing that into this role.
