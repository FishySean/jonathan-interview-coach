## Core principles

- **Qualified applications are scarce.** Hiring managers often cannot fill roles from inbound applications alone — they source on LinkedIn themselves.
- **Timing is a real selection factor.** Recruiters screen on a rolling basis; once interview slots fill, late applications may never be seen.
- **Recency of Open to Work signals commitment.** Sourcers and recruiters filter for people who *recently* set Open to Work — long-stale "open" profiles get ghosted more.
- **Hiring managers notice posts more than ATS.** Commenting or messaging under a manager's hiring post can get your profile seen when ATS buries you.
- **Search posts, not just job listings.** Managers habitually post open roles; those posters are eager to find candidates.

## Interview philosophy

This Short is **job-search / sourcing strategy**, not interview Q&A. Jonathan speaks as a hiring manager who had to source because inbound *qualified* candidates were insufficient. Standing out means matching how sourcers actually filter: early applications, recently refreshed Open to Work, and direct visibility on hiring posts.

## Evaluation criteria

From the *sourcer's* side, Jonathan reached for candidates who:

1. **Applied early** — Under the "under 10 applicants" / last-hour window before slots fill
2. **Looked recently Open to Work** — More committed than people who left the flag on indefinitely while employed
3. **Showed up on hiring posts** — Qualified people who messaged or commented under his posts (profile guaranteed seen if qualified)

Red flags (from his sourcing experience): ghosting when he messaged employed "Open to Work" people; applications that arrive after weekly interview slots are full.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Applying late and assuming the resume will still be screened.
- Leaving Open to Work on forever (gets deprioritized vs. recently refreshed status).
- Only searching job postings / relying on ATS instead of finding hiring *posts*.
- Waiting to be discovered instead of commenting/messaging under manager hiring posts when qualified.

## Follow-up questions

When a coachee's LinkedIn / apply strategy is weak, ask:

- "How soon after posting do you usually apply — hours or days?"
- "When did you last refresh Open to Work (off then on)?"
- "Are you searching `hiring` + job title under Posts, or only Jobs?"
- "Have you commented or messaged under a hiring manager's post for a role you're qualified for?"

## Answer frameworks

**Tip 1 — Filter early and filter fast:**
1. Filter to **under 10 applicants** (early bird)
2. Start with postings from the **last 24 hours**
3. In the LinkedIn URL, find `R86400` (seconds in 24 hours) → change to `3600` (last hour), `7200` (last two hours), etc.

**Tip 2 — Refresh Open to Work:**
1. Start of weekend: turn **off** Open to Work
2. End of weekend: turn it **back on**
3. Effect: profile looks recently refreshed; ranks higher when recruiters filter for recent Open to Work

**Tip 3 — Search posts, not job postings:**
1. Search keyword: `hiring` + your target job title
2. Set filter to **Posts** (not Jobs)
3. Message / comment under posts from people eager to hire

## Good vs bad examples

### Why managers source on LinkedIn

**Scenario:** Not enough qualified candidates from applications.

**Jonathan as hiring manager (verbatim):**

> When I was hiring for my team, I kept finding myself reaching out to these three kinds of candidates on LinkedIn. See, we could not get enough qualified candidates from applications. I know that's surprising, but the keyword here is qualified candidates. So outside of managing my team and my day-to-day job, I had to get on LinkedIn and source my own candidates.

---

### Timing: rolling screen vs late apply

**Scenario:** Candidate applies after interview slots for the week are filled.

**Bad outcome (verbatim):**

> Honestly, I used to wonder how many qualified candidates we missed because they were just a few hours too late. Maybe even a dream candidate like you, because I saw how common it was for recruiters to screen resumes on a rolling basis, meaning if they filled their five interview slots for the week and you're too late, you're too late. Your application won't ever be seen.

**Good — early + fast filters (verbatim):**

> And so if you want to be that early bird, it's super simple. Just set your filter to under 10 applicants.

> You want to start by filtering your search results to postings from the last 24 hours. Lots of people are doing this already, but we're going to take it one step further. Go to that URL in your browser and find the part that says R86400. That's the number of seconds in 24 hours. Change that number to 3600. Now you've got jobs posted in the last hour. 7200 gives you the last two hours and so on. And now you're faster than most other candidates.

---

### Open to Work: stale flag vs weekend refresh

**Scenario:** Sourcer filters Open to Work; many people leave it on while employed.

**Bad — ghosting pattern (verbatim):**

> But the problem is that a lot of people set themselves as open to work, even if they already have a job. And I get it, like we want to keep our options open, right? But when I reached out to them and said, hey, I know you have a job right now, but I think you'd be a good fit for my team here, I would get ghosted all the time.

**Good — recently refreshed status (verbatim):**

> But I found that when I filtered to people who had only recently set their status to open to work, they tended to be more committed to new opportunities. Makes sense, right? And I later found that recruiters often search in the exact same way. So if you want to be ranked higher, just do this. At the start of the weekend, turn off your Open2 status. Then at the end of the weekend, turn it back on. And now you've refreshed your profile.

---

### ATS vs comment under hiring post

**Scenario:** Getting noticed by the hiring manager.

**Bad:** Profile lost in ATS; never seen.

**Good — posts + comment/message (verbatim):**

> When I was hiring, it was almost a habit for me to post open positions on LinkedIn for my team. And of course, people who messaged me or commented under my posts would capture my attention. I'm not saying I replied to every single one, but if you were qualified, commenting under my post would guarantee that I would see your profile, instead of it getting lost in our ATS, right?

> So if you want to get noticed too, then do this. Instead of searching for jobs, search the keyword hiring plus your target job title, and then set your filter to posts instead of jobs. And boom, you're going to have a long list of posters who are eager to find candidates.
