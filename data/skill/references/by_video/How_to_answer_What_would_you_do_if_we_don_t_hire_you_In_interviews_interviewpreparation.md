## Core principles

- **“What would you do if we don’t hire you?” is a trap** — easy to sound arrogant, desperate, or uninterested; it does **not** mean you failed.
- They want **genuine interest + resilience + confidence in your fit**.
- Reframe rejection: **“no just means not yet.”**
- Close with confidence you can bring value **right away** and hope neither side has to wait.

## Interview philosophy

End-of-interview hypotheticals test emotional regulation and maturity: can you hold disappointment without stalking, without shrugging, and without abandoning fit confidence?

## Evaluation criteria

Signals they read:

- Honest disappointment without meltdown
- Specific excitement (reason A)
- Respect for the company either way
- Forward motion: improve + apply X skill / Y achievement / Z passion
- Desire to stay connected
- Confidence you’ve already proven value in the discussion

## Common candidate mistakes

**Bad answers he opens with (verbatim tone):**

> This is my dream company. So I will keep applying and emailing you every day.

> Uh, try to find another job. What do you want from me?

- Sounding arrogant, desperate, or uninterested
- Panicking as if the question = failure

## Follow-up questions

Self-check before answering:

- “Did I name a real reason I’m excited (A)?”
- “Did I show resilience without desperation?”
- “Did I restate X skill / Y achievement / Z passion?”
- “Did I close with confidence in fit without begging?”

## Answer frameworks

### Step-by-step template

1. Honestly, I’d feel disappointed.
2. Excited because of **A**.
3. Still respect the company / great work.
4. If not this time: keep improving; apply **X skill**, **Y achievement**, **Z area**.
5. Hope to stay connected — **no = not yet**.
6. Confident I can bring value right away; trust I’ve proven it; hope we make it happen this time.
