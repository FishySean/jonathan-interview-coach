## Core principles

- **Leading meetings as an ESL speaker is a scriptable skill** — Jonathan models a full meeting from warm open → agenda → handoff → facilitation → redirect → time check → async next steps.
- **Reward on-time arrivals** with a peek at the agenda while waiting for others.
- **Pass clearly to owners** (“Jen, over to you”) and handle mutes / dropouts calmly.
- **Facilitate discussion:** invite spitballing; acknowledge points; challenge margin/churn tradeoffs with options.
- **Table off-topic ideas** politely (“next week’s sync / take it offline”).
- **Clarify, time-check, capture next steps, distribute async** for review/comment.

## Interview philosophy

Workplace leadership for ESL professionals is demonstrated in meeting control: warmth + structure + inclusive facilitation + decisive redirects + crisp close. The Short is a live demo of language patterns, not abstract theory.

## Evaluation criteria

Signals of strong meeting leadership (from the demo):

- Warm rapport without losing control of time.
- Visible agenda and clear sequence (dashboard → timelines → action items).
- Clean handoffs and mute recovery.
- Discussion management: acknowledge → concern → ask for alternatives.
- Scope control: table ideas that won’t move the needle.
- Close: time check, next steps captured, async distribution, thanks.

## Common candidate mistakes

Implied by what the script avoids:

- Starting cold with no rapport or agenda peek.
- Unclear handoffs; ignoring mute / “are you still with us?” moments awkwardly.
- Letting discussion sprawl without redirect.
- Ending without captured next steps or async follow-up.

## Follow-up questions

Self-review after leading a meeting:

- “Did late joiners still get a clear agenda?”
- “Did I hand off with a named owner and context?”
- “When ideas wouldn’t move the needle, did I table them kindly?”
- “Did I leave with next steps I’ll distribute async?”

## Answer frameworks

### Meeting arc (HAS-like structure of the demo)

1. **Warm open + wait buffer** — season small talk; thank early joiners; share agenda peek.
2. **Kickoff** — full agenda; jump in; name first owner.
3. **Facilitate** — unmute help; open discussion; acknowledge + challenge; clarify.
4. **Redirect / table** — off-needle ideas → next week / offline.
5. **Close** — time check; next steps; async distribute; thanks.

## Good vs bad examples

This Short is almost entirely a **good** live script. Contrasts are implicit (chaotic meeting vs this control). Reproduce clusters below as teachable beats.

### Warm open while waiting

**Good (verbatim):**

> Hey everyone. Thanks for joining. I cannot believe it's already September. Has this year just flown by? Oh, 100%. I'm right there with you getting into the spirit. We're going to give folks a couple minutes to join. So in the meantime, thanks to all of you here for joining on time. You get a quick peek at today's agenda as my thanks to you. So I'm going to share my screen with the agenda. If you can't see my screen, just let me know.

### Kickoff + handoff

**Good (verbatim):**

> Okay, looks like everyone's here. We've got lots to cover today, so let's jump right in. First up is the new dashboard review. Then a quick check in on timelines and finally some action items. To kick things off, I'm going to pass it over to Jen, who is a resident data rock star, and she's going to introduce the dashboard. Jen, over to you. Sorry to interrupt, Jen. I think you're on mute.

### Facilitate + challenge

**Good (verbatim):**

> Thanks, Jen. Let's open this up now for some discussion. Any initial thoughts from the team? We're just spitballing here, so feel free to jump in. I see your point. Lowering prices could be a great incentive to retain customers. Although I am concerned that the head to our margin might offset any gains in retention. So could we consider other options first?

### Table / clarify / reconnect

**Good (verbatim):**

> That's an interesting idea, Jerry. But unless I'm missing something that won't move the needle on churn, right? So could we table that for next week's sink or take it offline? Sorry, Lynn, could you clarify what you meant by funnel optimization? Lynn, are you still with us? Or was it just me? Oh, you're back. I can hear you loud and clear.

### Close

**Good (verbatim):**

> All right, quick time check. We're almost at time. So I'm going to wrap us up shortly. Here are the next steps that I captured, and I will distribute these in our group after the call so we can review and comment a sink. Thanks again, everyone. Great energy, great takeaways as usual, and we'll talk soon.
