## Core principles

- **The crazy job market isn’t always “you” — it’s your filters** (or lack of smart ones).
- **Don’t compete with hundreds of applicants** when you can filter to under 10 and get noticed.
- **Math of attention:** 1/100 ≈ 1% vs 1/10 = 10% odds of being noticed.
- **Recency matters:** Recruiters close postings once they have enough interview candidates — apply early (24h, then last hour).
- **Pair “under 10 applicants” + “posted last hour”** to be first in line.

## Interview philosophy

Getting interviews is partly a discovery/ops problem before it’s a resume problem. Top candidates game LinkedIn job filters for low competition and freshness so recruiters see them while the funnel is still open.

## Evaluation criteria

Self-application strategy metrics:

- **Applicant count:** Prefer postings with under 10 applicants.
- **Freshness:** Last 24 hours; ideally last hour (URL tweak `3600`).
- **Notice probability:** Favor 10% (1/10) over 1% (1/100) math.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Applying to roles already at 100+ applications an hour after posting.
- Not using LinkedIn “under 10 applicants” filter.
- Ignoring “day posted” / last 24 hours — arriving after recruiters already have enough pipeline.
- Competing in saturated pools instead of filtering for first-mover advantage.

## Follow-up questions

- “Did I set LinkedIn to under 10 applicants?”
- “Did I also filter to last 24 hours — or tighten the URL to last hour (3600)?”
- “Am I applying where I’m one of few, or one of hundreds?”

## Answer frameworks

### LinkedIn job-hunt SOP

1. Filter: **under 10 applicants**.
2. Filter: **day posted → last 24 hours**.
3. Advanced: In the webpage URL, find the 24-hour filter string; change the number to **3600** (last hour).
4. Pair under-10 + last-hour → first in line.

## Good vs bad examples

### Saturated posting vs filtered hunt

**Scenario:** Daily applications; role posted one hour ago already has 100+ apps.

**Bad (verbatim comic despair):**

> Oh wait, this one was posted one hour ago and over a hundred applications. Maybe I should just try to get a job at Chick-fil-A.

**Misread of “filters” (verbatim banter):**

> My filters! I don't use filters. I'm all natty, bruh.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Stop trying to compete with hundreds of other applicants. On LinkedIn jobs, set your filter to under 10 applicants and voila! You will get a list of jobs where you're actually more likely to get noticed… So one out of a hundred is one percent. But one out of ten is ten percent.

---

### 24 hours vs last hour

**Scenario:** Following up after under-10 filter.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Go to the day posted filter and set it to the last 24 hours. Top candidates are already doing this because they know that recruiters will close job postings as soon as they get enough candidates to interview. So we're going to take this one step further. Go to the webpage URL and find this string. That is the 24 hour filter. Change that number to 3600 and now you're seeing jobs posted in the last hour. Pair that with the under 10 applicants and you're going to be first in line.
