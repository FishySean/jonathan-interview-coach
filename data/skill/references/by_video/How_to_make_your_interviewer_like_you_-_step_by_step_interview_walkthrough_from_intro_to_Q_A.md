## Core principles

- Skills and right answers aren’t enough — if they **don’t like you**, offers are hard.
- **Five opportunities** for Chinese candidates with Western interviewers: greeting, small talk, listening etiquette, clarification phrasing, close + thank-you email.
- Small talk is a **verbal handshake** — give something to converse on.
- Follow the interviewer’s tone; let them end small talk.
- Thank-you emails must be **personalized**, not generic copy-paste.

## Interview philosophy

Western interviewers decide early whether you’re enjoyable to talk to. Over-nodding / “yes yes okay I got it” while they’re explaining can read as impatience (“shut up”). Demanding “repeat that / I don’t understand” also lands poorly. Listening callbacks at the close and personalized follow-up emails create momentum.

## Evaluation criteria

- Warm, brief self-share in greeting (not ice-cold autopilot).
- Small talk that offers a conversational hook.
- Listening: confirm once; ask for rephrase politely if needed.
- Close references something discussed.
- Thank-you email personalized (not generic).

## Common candidate mistakes

- Greeting: “I'm good, how are you?” only — cold start.
- Commute: “oh, my commute was good” — nothing to build on.
- Vigorous nodding / “yes, yes, okay, I got it” while interviewer speaks.
- “Can you repeat that?” / “I don't understand” (impatient/demanding).
- Thinking “we can end here” means no more impression work.
- Generic thank-you emails (Jonathan: received hundreds; does not appreciate them).

## Good vs bad examples

### Greeting (“How are you?”)

**Bad:** “I'm good, how are you?”

**Good:**

> I'm great. I'm excited for our chat and I'm grateful for your time. I figure it's a particularly busy time of year for you.

> I couldn't be better. I'm excited for this because I've been a loyal client for years and it's a real treat to meet you and learn more about the team.

### Small talk (commute)

**Bad:** “oh, my commute was good.”

**Good:**

> the commute was easy. I like how close the office is to the station and this district seems pretty fun, isn't it?

### Clarification

**Bad:** “can you repeat that?” / “I don't understand.”

**Good:**

> could you please repeat or rephrase your question? I want to make sure that I can fully address it.

### Close

**Good:**

> thank you for taking the time to meet with me today. I especially appreciated our discussion of your team's goals for the next year. And I'm looking forward to exploring how I can help.
