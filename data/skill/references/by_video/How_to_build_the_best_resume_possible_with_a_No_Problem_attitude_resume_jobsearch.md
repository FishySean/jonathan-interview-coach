## Core principles

- **This resume doesn’t need more experience — it needs a “no-problem” attitude.**
- **No skills? No problem.** Put it on the resume first, then go learn it (e.g. weekend Python course) — then you’re not lying; ATS may screen you out if you omit basics.
- **You don’t need permission to claim basic proficiency.**
- **No work experience? No problem.** Get it now: TheForge-style projects/certs (BCG, JP Morgan, PepsiCo), Upwork freelance, side projects built in the open.
- **All experience in China? No problem.** Remove locations — resume isn’t a passport; let experience speak louder than address.
- **Weak bullets? No problem.** Golden formula: **Skill, action, and result** — turn responsibilities into achievements; estimate numbers if needed (resumes aren’t legal audits).
- **You’ll be limited by skills or beliefs — never let it be beliefs.**

## Interview philosophy

Jonathan treats resume blockers as belief/ops problems, not destiny. Ship learning and proof fast; optimize for ATS + human impact storytelling.

## Evaluation criteria

Resume readers / ATS:

- Skills present so you aren’t auto-screened
- Real-ish experience lines (projects, freelance, open builds)
- Location noise minimized when it biases
- Bullets with skill + action + quantified result
- Evidence you measure success

## Common candidate mistakes

- Waiting for permission / more years before claiming basic skills
- Omitting learnable skills and getting ATS-killed
- Leaving China locations when they drown out experience
- Responsibility-only bullets without results/numbers
- Letting beliefs (“I can’t”) limit before skills do

## Follow-up questions

- “What skill can I put down and learn this weekend?”
- “Where can I get a project/cert/client line this week?”
- “Do locations on my resume help or hurt?”
- “Does each bullet = skill + action + result with a number (even estimated)?”

## Answer frameworks

### No-problem playbook

1. Skill gap → write basic proficiency → learn immediately
2. Experience gap → Forge / Upwork / public side project
3. Location bias → remove locations
4. Weak points → Skill + action + result; estimate metrics

## Good vs bad examples

### Skill claim

**Bad belief:** Can’t list Python until “fully” skilled.

**Good (verbatim):**

> No skills? No problem. Put it on your resume first, then go learn it. It's that simple. Don't know Python? Then go take a Python course over the weekend. Now you're not lying. You've got to write that skill down because if you don't, chances are the ATS will screen you out before a human even reads it. You don't need permission to claim basic proficiency in a skill.

### Experience manufacture

**Good (verbatim):**

> No work experience? No problem. Go get it for yourself right now. There are sites built for this like theforge.com where you can complete real projects, gain real certifications and have real companies like BCG, JP Morgan and PepsiCo to your resume and you can do it in minutes. Or freelance on Upwork. One client, one project can be its own work experience. Or start a side project and post it online.

### Location

**Good (verbatim):**

> All your experiences in China? No problem. Remove the locations from your resume. Your resume is not a passport, so let your experiences speak louder than your address.

### Bullet rewrite

**Bad → Good (verbatim):**

> Used Python to clean data pipelines turns into built-in automated data cleaning pipeline with Python to automate validation from disparate data sources, reducing manual reporting time by 40%.

**Numbers (verbatim):**

> Don't have any numbers? No problem. Just estimate. Resumes are not legal documents. Nobody's going to audit whether you actually increased efficiency by 20% or 2%. The important thing is to show you know how to measure success and to demonstrate impact.

**Close (verbatim):**

> In your career and in life, you will be limited by one of two things. Your skills or your beliefs. Never let it be your beliefs.
