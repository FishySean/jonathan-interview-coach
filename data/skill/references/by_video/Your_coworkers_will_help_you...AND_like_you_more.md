## Core principles

- Easiest real workplace relationships: **don’t go quiet after someone helps you.**
- “Thank you” then silence misses the moment a connection could be born — helpers wonder if advice was useful.
- They don’t need a long thank-you — they need to know **they made a difference.**
- Follow-up appreciation → they like you more (**Benjamin Franklin effect**: helping → believe they like you; show help mattered → reinforce “I give good advice / help people who grow”).
- Simple message: tried XYZ → it helped → how it made things better → they want to help again.

## Interview philosophy

Relationship capital is closed loops. Same pattern helps networking and “how do you work with others” stories: close the feedback loop after help.

## Evaluation criteria

- Closed loop after help (not thank-and-vanish)
- Specific outcome of their advice
- Reinforces helper’s identity as useful

## Common candidate mistakes

- Sincere thank you then radio silence
- Too busy to report back
- Long empty thanks without “here’s how it helped”

## Follow-up questions

- “Who helped me recently — did I report the outcome?”
- “Can I name XYZ + how it got better in one message?”

## Answer frameworks

### Follow-up appreciation

> Last time you told me to try XYZ. I actually did and it helped so much. Here's how it made things better.

## Good vs bad examples

### Thank-and-vanish vs loop close

**Bad (verbatim):**

> someone gives you a helpful suggestion… you say, thank you… and then that's it. You're just too busy, you move on… They might still be wondering… Did she try what I suggested? Was that even useful

**Effect (verbatim):**

> When you follow up with appreciation, they're not just happy to hear it, they actually like you more… Benjamin Franklin effect… You're giving them a story about themselves, like I'm someone who gives good advice. I help people who grow. I want to keep supporting this person.

**Good message (verbatim):**

> Last time you told me to try XYZ. I actually did and it helped so much. Here's how it made things better. That moment sticks… it makes them want to help you again.
