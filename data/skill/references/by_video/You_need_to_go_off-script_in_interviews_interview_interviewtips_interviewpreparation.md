## Core principles

- Rejected because answers are **too perfect** / AI-polished — they know that’s not you.
- Interviewers aren’t only ticking boxes — **picturing you in the role** (e.g. “I need another Emma”).
- In the age of AI, when everyone sounds smart, **smart stops mattering** — offers go to those who prove they understand and can solve the interviewer’s needs.
- **Show, don’t just tell** who you are.
- Hole poked in answer? Don’t freeze — **revise live** (growth mindset in real time > saying “I have a growth mindset”).
- Leadership: don’t just claim it — **be a leader in the interview** (guide the intro: A/B/C → most interested in C?).
- Treat interviewer as future colleague, not examiner — conversation, not interrogation.

## Interview philosophy

Hiring is matching a mental image of a person who solves needs under pressure. Authenticity + adaptive collaboration beat GPT-perfect scripts.

## Evaluation criteria

- Real voice vs polished script
- Understands interviewer’s needs
- Live revise when challenged
- Guides conversation (leadership demo)
- Colleague energy

## Common candidate mistakes

- Over-rehearsed AI answers
- “Right answers” without need-solving proof
- Freezing when interviewer challenges
- Claiming growth mindset / leadership without demonstrating

## Follow-up questions

- “Am I showing need-solving, or reciting polish?”
- “If they poke a hole, can I revise live?”
- “In intro, am I guiding toward what they care about most?”

## Answer frameworks

### Live revise

> hey, that's a great point. Thank you. Here's how I'd revise my answer to include your feedback.

### Guided intro

> I've done a B and C. And from what I've read about this role, I think you'd be most interested in C. Is that right? I can go deeper if that would help.
