## Core principles

- **Every day you train your manager how to treat you — so train them well.**
- Immediate 10pm replies → always on call; “no problem” to last-minute dumps → poor planning has no consequences; smiling through unfair criticism → disrespect is OK.
- Boundaries aren’t selfish — they’re the **manual** for how others should treat you; write it in **their language**.
- **Manager Matrix** (for Chinese professionals Jonathan coaches): two dimensions — need for **control** + reliance on **structure** → four styles.

## Interview philosophy

Managing up = untraining bad norms and matching the boss’s safety/control needs. Once you see what drives them, you stop taking it personally and manage with confidence.

## Evaluation criteria

- Are your behaviors training respect or exploitation?
- Do updates/structure match their style (before they ask vs don’t over-detail)?
- Boundaries held consistently after conflict?

## Common candidate mistakes

- Always-on replies to night messages.
- Absorbing last-minute scope with “no problem.”
- Apologizing through disrespect (including swearing on Slack).
- Waiting for a hands-off pleaser to set direction.
- Slowing a visionary with too many details.
- Hiding mistakes from a micromanager.

## Follow-up questions

Self-check: Which matrix type is my manager? What am I currently training them to expect?

## Answer frameworks

### Manager Matrix (control × structure)

| Type | Traits | How to win trust |
|------|--------|------------------|
| **Micromanager** | Needs control/every detail/update; safety via control | Update before they ask; never hide mistakes; align early on what good looks like |
| **Visionary** | Big vision, little structure; moves fast, changes direction | Don’t slow with too many details; bring structure; turn ideas into next steps |
| **Team builder** | People + process; feedback, check-ins, teamwork | Support the structure; help build the team |
| **Hands-off pleaser** | Kind, avoids conflict; praise often, rare boundaries | Don’t wait for direction; gently create structure yourself |

No type is best; people don’t fit one box cleanly.

## Good vs bad examples

### Scenario: Disrespect / night Slack

**Bad training:** Apologize; stay always-on; accept F-bombs.

**Untraining Jonathan did:** Stopped weekend replies; pushed back on last-minute scope; defended work when he disagreed — first time caused a big argument; afterward disrespect stopped.

**Why it matters:** “every apology was just training him that I was okay with disrespect.”
