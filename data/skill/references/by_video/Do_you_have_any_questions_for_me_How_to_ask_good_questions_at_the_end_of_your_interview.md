## Core principles

- **Never say you have no questions** — ask something, anything.
- **End-of-interview Qs are a bonus round** — most candidates put in minimal effort; thoughtful questions turn Q&A into a conversation.
- Upgrade from generic culture/day questions to **specific, value-tied, role-impact, growth, and closing-clarification** questions.

## Interview philosophy

“Do you have any questions for me?” is not a formality — it’s a chance to stand out by showing preparation, curiosity about real work, and confidence to check fit both ways (including clarifying your own candidacy).

## Evaluation criteria

Strong closing questions signal:

- You listened (tie to a named core value).
- You care about first-90-days impact (“biggest rock”).
- You care about growth path with concrete examples.
- You seek success criteria for the role.
- You’re willing to close gaps: “anything I can clarify… strongest fit?”

## Common candidate mistakes

- Saying you have no questions.
- Minimal-effort generics only (or freezing: “Quick. Ask something. Anything.”).
- Treating the segment as throwaway instead of a conversation.

## Follow-up questions

Jonathan’s upgraded versions (use these):

- Culture → value-in-action
- Typical day → biggest rock in first 90 days
- Professional development → example of someone who grew from this role
- Ideal candidate → characteristics of people who succeeded
- Next steps → clarify anything about my background for strongest fit

## Answer frameworks

### Upgrade pattern

| Weak / common ask | Stronger ask |
|-------------------|--------------|
| What does your culture look like? | I noticed one of your core values is X — is there a time that value came to life for you? |
| What does a typical day look like? | What’s the biggest rock you’d like this candidate to move in the first 90 days? |
| What does professional development look like? | Can you show an example of someone who started in this role and grew within the company? |
| What are you looking for in the ideal candidate? | What are the characteristics of people who have succeeded in this role? |
| What are the next steps? | Based on our conversation so far, is there anything I can clarify about my background to ensure that I'm the strongest fit? |

## Good vs bad examples

### Panic / no questions vs bonus-round conversation

**Scenario:** Interviewer asks “Do you have any questions for me?”

**Bad (verbatim panic):**

> Quick. Ask something. Anything. Just don't say you have no questions.

**Mindset (verbatim):**

> Okay, this is the bonus round. Most candidates are putting in minimal effort here. So time for me to stand out by asking thoughtful questions that turn this from a Q&A into a conversation.

### Generic → upgraded pairs (verbatim)

**Bad → Good:**

> What does your culture look like? → I noticed one of your core values is customer empathy. Is there a time where that value came to life for you?

> What does a typical day look like? → What's the biggest rock that you'd like this candidate to move in the first 90 days?

> What does professional development look like here? → Can you show an example of someone who started in this role and grew within the company?

> What are you looking for in the ideal candidate? → What are the characteristics of people who have succeeded in this role?

> What are the next steps in the process? → Based on our conversation so far, is there anything I can clarify about my background to ensure that I'm the strongest fit?
