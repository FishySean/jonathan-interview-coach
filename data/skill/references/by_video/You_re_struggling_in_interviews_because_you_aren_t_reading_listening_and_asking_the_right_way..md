## Core principles

- Struggle usually isn’t economy / “stupid interviewer” / ESL — it’s **not reading, not listening, not asking**.
- Don’t try to be **everything to everyone**; more-qualified candidates try to be **something to someone**.
- **Read** JDs critically like the interviewer; **listen** for company/role fit (not “I’m the best”); **ask** throughout to uncover pain — not only at the end.
- Ask **good** questions, not generic overused ones; best candidates open more conversation on pain and how they address it.

## Interview philosophy

Hiring managers reveal what they need (stack, culture, trust, build-from-zero). Candidates who memorize scripts wait for the interviewer to finish; winners decode intent and mirror context.

## Evaluation criteria

- Thoughtful answer to “most/least excited about this job”
- Self-assessment: strengths/weaknesses vs role; goals fit; dream-candidate from HM POV
- Decode intent before answering; mirror their challenge/goal language
- Questions throughout that surface pain points

## Common candidate mistakes

- Blaming economy/interviewer/ESL instead of read/listen/ask gaps.
- Being everything to everyone.
- Scanning JD for skills only — freezing on excitement questions.
- Saying “I’m the best candidate” instead of “best for this company/role.”
- Waiting to recite memorized answers.
- Saving all questions for the end; asking generic questions.

## Follow-up questions

### Read like the HM

- “What are my strengths and weaknesses based on this role?”
- “What are my long and short term goals? And how do they fit with this role?”
- “And if I were the hiring manager, what would my dream candidate look like?”

### Listen

Before answering: “What problem or goal are they testing with this question?”

### Ask (Jonathan loves hearing these — list referenced but not fully enumerated as three verbatim Qs in the spoken transcript beyond the principle)

Use questions that uncover needs throughout; then explore pain and show how you address it.

## Answer frameworks

1. **Read:** critical JD questions above.
2. **Listen:** decode intent → answer to solve that problem; mirror their words about challenges/goals.
3. **Ask:** throughout the interview whenever you need more needs — not a closing chore.

## Good vs bad examples

### HM question: most/least excited about the job

**Bad:** Freeze — hadn’t researched responsibilities.

**Good:** Thoughtful answer showing you understand what they care about and why the role matters to you.

### Building DE team 0→100

**What HM wanted:** work with stack, live culture, build trust fast with leaders — explained every interview.

**Bad:** Wait for him to finish → recite memorized answers.

**Good:** Self-starter who builds trust quickly; weave his challenge language into answers.
