## Core principles

- **One strategy to sound smarter with leaders: SCQA** — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer (McKinsey executive communication).
- Leaders don’t care about vocabulary — they care whether ideas are **clear, structured, and help them decide faster**.
- **Stop trying to impress. Start helping them decide.**
- Unstructured updates get leaders to “pull out the phone”; structured SCQA gets them to stop and listen.

## Interview philosophy

Executive communication is decision support. Jonathan’s manager enforced SCQA on the team; unstructured talk = ignored. Same skill transfers to interview “project status / how do you communicate” answers.

## Evaluation criteria

Good update:

- Clear **situation** (where we are)
- Named **complication** (what changed)
- Explicit **question/decision** (what leadership must choose)
- Crisp **answer/recommendation** with rationale

Poor update: rambling difficulty, late nights, vague “figure something out.”

## Common candidate mistakes

- Unstructured status dumps
- Leading with struggle/effort instead of decision
- Trying to impress with words instead of clarity
- Soft “maybe we need to…” without a recommendation

## Follow-up questions

- “What’s my Situation in one line?”
- “What’s the Complication?”
- “What Question does leadership need to answer?”
- “What’s my Answer / recommendation?”

## Answer frameworks

### SCQA

1. **Situation**
2. **Complication**
3. **Question**
4. **Answer** (recommendation)

## Good vs bad examples

### “Is your project on track for Q4?”

**Bad (verbatim):**

> yeah, we've been working on that, but it's been tough with a few things getting added to scope. And the team has been pulling late nights to catch up. We're struggling. I was thinking maybe we need to reprioritize our features, maybe ask for more support. We'd probably have to figure something out soon.

**Challenge (verbatim):**

> If you were the director, would you feel good about that update?

**Good SCQA (verbatim):**

> We're off track for Q4, delivery. The issue is that several new features were added mid-cycle. So we need to decide whether to reduce scope or add more resources. My recommendation is to reallocate headcount temporarily because, see, leaders, they don't care about your vocabulary. They care whether your ideas are clear, structured and help them to make decisions faster. So stop trying to impress. Start helping them decide.

*(Note: ASR may have glued the teaching closer into the sample; the SCQA body is: off track → new features mid-cycle → decide reduce scope vs add resources → recommend temporary headcount reallocation.)*

**Origin story (verbatim):**

> A few years ago, I remember my manager pulled me aside after this presentation that I made to our VP… John, you're smart, but we need to work on your executive communication… Like the moment we started communicating in any unstructured way, he'd immediately pull out his phone and pretend to start scrolling through it… This method that he used is called SCQA…
