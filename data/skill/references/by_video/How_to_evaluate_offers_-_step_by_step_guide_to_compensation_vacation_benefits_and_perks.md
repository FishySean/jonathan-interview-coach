## Core principles

- Evaluate **total comp**, not just base salary.
- Smart candidates **don’t chase offers — they choose them**.
- Lock down big stuff first: **comp, title, level** — then perks.
- Recruiter “dream equity” stories are unreliable; verify vesting/cliff/refresh.
- Discretionary bonus → assume **zero** until proven.
- Level/title on the offer must match what you interviewed for; get promotion promises **in writing** (they usually won’t).
- “Titles don’t matter here” is a **red flag** — titles follow you on your resume.

## Interview philosophy

Offers are negotiable packages: equity mechanics, bonus history, retirement match, one-time bonuses (signing/relocation), level, title, insurance quality, vacation reality, real perks. Naïveté (signing wrong level) creates lasting damage.

## Evaluation criteria

Checklist:

1. Equity: vesting schedule, cliff, refresh; don’t buy “10x story” alone
2. Bonus: target %, criteria, historical payout; discretionary ≈ 0
3. Retirement matching
4. One-time bonuses (signing/relocation) + clawback windows
5. Job **level** matches interview track
6. Job **title** matches JD; negotiate title if needed
7. Insurance substance (not just “we have benefits”)
8. Vacation days; “unlimited” → ask how much HR actually took
9. Real perks (cash/time value), not ping-pong

## Common candidate mistakes

- Fixating on base only.
- Believing recruiter equity growth stories.
- Assuming discretionary bonuses pay out.
- Not reading level on the offer (senior interview → entry offer).
- Accepting verbal “we’ll promote you in a year” without writing.
- Ignoring title mismatches; believing titles don’t matter.
- Treating unlimited PTO as unlimited.
- Prioritizing kitchen/ping-pong over comp/title/level.

## Follow-up questions

Ask recruiters/HR:

- Vesting schedule, cliff, refresh policies?
- Bonus target %, criteria, historical payout rates?
- Retirement match %?
- Why does offer level/title differ from the JD/interview?
- Can you put the promotion timeline in writing?
- How much vacation did you (HR) take last year? (unlimited PTO guide)

## Answer frameworks

**Order:** total comp mechanics → level/title → insurance → vacation → perks (last).

**Negotiation notes:** signing/relocation bonuses possible (Jonathan cites up to ~40k helped); often clawed back if you leave within a year. Titles can be more valuable long-term than a small salary bump.

## Good vs bad examples

### Equity sales pitch

**Bad (recruiter):** “we pay less, but our stock has 10x in the past few years. Imagine how much more can grow.”

**Outcome Jonathan cites:** stock dropped ~50% months later, still not recovered.

### Level mismatch

**Bad:** Interviewed senior researcher, signed entry-level without careful read → stuck/devastated.

**Good:** Always double-check level matches what you interviewed for.

### Unlimited vacation

**Bad:** Believe “unlimited.”

**Good:** It’s never unlimited; employees often take less; ask HR what they took last year.

### Real perks (examples)

- Uber: $300/month rides/eats
- Shopify: $2,000 baby bonus
- Another: $5,000/year personal development
- Another: extra day off every long weekend (~two weeks)

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Evaluation thesis

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Step-by-step offer evaluation (comp, vacation, benefits, perks).
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Offer negotiation / evaluation (post-interview).
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> Hint, if they say it's discretionary, assume it's zero.

> Oh, and if anyone promises you, we'll start you lower, but we'll promote you in a year. Ask them to put it in writing. They won't.

> Oh, and if they say titles don't matter here, take note of that red flag. Titles always matter. After you leave, you have to live with that on your resume. They don't.

> It's pretty simple. More is better, but be careful if you see the words unlimited vacation. It is never unlimited.

> So yeah, perks are great, but always lock down the big stuff first, like comp title level. And the next time you evaluate an offer, remember, smart candidates don't chase offers. They choose them.
