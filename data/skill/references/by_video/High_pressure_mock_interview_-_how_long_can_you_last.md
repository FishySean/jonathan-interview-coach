## Core principles

- This Short is a **live high-pressure mock**: Jonathan plays the interviewer and shows the follow-ups that break weak answers.
- Highlights ≠ chronological resume dump — **connect the dots** for *this* position.
- “Why us” must be **specific** to culture/business/story — not “industry leader” (applies to competitors).
- Impact > responsibilities; quantify then defend **your** role vs the team.
- Leadership stories need **real work stakes**, not school projects (“projects are toys”).
- Smooth stories get stress-tested: alternatives, exact words used to persuade, “what if they didn’t agree?”
- “I read the news” fails without **last thing read** + **implications for our business**.

## Interview philosophy

Strong interviewers won’t accept generic, responsibility-lists, team-credit mush, or vague industry awareness. They interrupt, reframe, and demand specificity until the answer is evidence-based and personal.

## Evaluation criteria

Signals this mock is testing:

- Relevance filter on intro
- Company-specific motivation
- Personal impact (not job description)
- Ownership clarity when numbers are team outcomes
- Real stakes + approach choice + persuasion specifics + contingency
- Current, business-linked industry awareness

## Common candidate mistakes

(Implied by Jonathan’s interruptions)

- Chronological resume repeat
- “You’re an industry leader” as why-us
- Listing analyst responsibilities instead of impact
- Claiming team metrics without clarifying personal role (“supporting”)
- Using school projects for leadership
- Vague “common ground” without exact words said
- Only describing the smooth path — no plan if they disagreed
- “I read the news” with nothing recent or relevant

## Follow-up questions

Verbatim interviewer probes from the mock (use these when coaching / stress-testing):

- “Could you please just give me the highlights that are relevant to this position? Help me connect the dots here.”
- “So why would you pick our company in particular? Anything specific to our culture, our business, our story?”
- “Can we please skip the responsibilities and just talk about the impact of your work? How did you change your team, organization, your company for the better?”
- “Did you do this all by yourself? … What exactly was your role here?”
- “Can you tell me about a time when you took leadership responsibility beyond your scope?”
- “Can we not talk about school projects here? … real world examples with real work stakes…”
- “Why did you choose that approach instead of the alternatives?”
- “When others didn't agree with you at first, how did you bring them around?”
- “What did you actually say to them to reach that common ground? Be specific, please.”
- “What if they didn't agree with you? What would you have done differently?”
- “What's the last thing that you read in the news? … What implications do you think that has for our business here?”

## Answer frameworks

When under this pressure:

1. Intro → 3–4 role-relevant highlights that connect dots.
2. Why us → one culture/business/story detail only they have.
3. Experience → impact first (metric + who changed); then your personal slice.
4. Leadership → beyond-scope, real stakes; approach vs alternatives; exact persuasion language; Plan B if blocked.
5. Industry → recent specific input + implication for *this* company.

## Good vs bad examples

### Why us

**Bad (implied):** “we're a leader in the industry.”

**Interviewer push:**

> Well, it sounds like you could say that about any of our competitors. So why would you pick our company in particular?

### Impact vs responsibilities

**Bad:** Explaining what an analyst does.

**Push:** Skip responsibilities → impact on team/org/company.

### Team metric ownership

**Candidate path in mock:** Helped reduce churn 12% → pressed → “supporting” → interviewer asks for a time *you* moved the needle, not your team.

### School vs real stakes

**Push:**

> Projects are toys, right? Can you give me real world examples with real work stakes and challenges that have actual consequences?
