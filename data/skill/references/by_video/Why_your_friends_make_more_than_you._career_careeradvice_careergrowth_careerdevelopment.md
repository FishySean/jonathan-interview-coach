## Core principles

- Lie: **work harder, earn more** — hardest workers (construction, nurses, taxi) don’t automatically earn more.
- **Wealth ≠ effort.** It’s **how far you stretch × how long you stay.**
- **Stretch:** educated risks — visible risky projects, roles you’re not ready for, industries nobody believes in.
- **Stay:** survive fog of uncertainty when others turn back before breakthrough.
- Example: Meta AI hire at $200M vs Tim Cook $75M — not twice as smart/hardworking; stretched early on AI and stayed building skills until world caught up.
- Next salary jump won’t come from another late night — from the **risk you’re most afraid to take.** Leap despite no guaranteed payoff.

## Interview philosophy

Career compensation narratives: interview stories should show stretch+stay, not only grind. “Hardworking” alone doesn’t differentiate.

## Evaluation criteria

- Educated risk evidence
- Persistence through uncertainty
- Not pure hours/effort as the value thesis

## Common candidate mistakes

- Believing late-night emails + slide polish = promotion
- Avoiding visible risk / “not ready yet” roles
- Turning back in the fog before breakthrough

## Follow-up questions

- “What’s the risky visible bet I’m avoiding?”
- “Where have I stretched and stayed long enough for compounding?”
- “Am I optimizing effort or stretch×stay?”

## Answer frameworks

### Stretch × Stay

1. Name an educated risk (project/role/industry)  
2. Show staying through uncertainty  
3. Outcome when world caught up (or learning so far)  

## Good vs bad examples

### Harder work vs stretch×stay

**Lie (verbatim):**

> work harder, earn more… the hardest working people… Why aren't they earning more?… wealth is not a function of effort. It is a function of how far you can stretch, multiplied by how long you can stay.

**Stretch/stay (verbatim):**

> Stretching means taking educated risks, taking the risky project with high visibility, fighting for the role that you're not ready for yet or pushing into an industry that nobody believes in. And staying means surviving the fog of uncertainty when nothing is guaranteed and most people turn back right before the breakthrough.

**Close (verbatim):**

> Your next salary jump won't come from another late night. It'll come from the risk that you're most afraid to take… you've still got to leap.
