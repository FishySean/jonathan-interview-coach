## Core principles

- **The more you try to be liked at work, the more they ignore you.**
- Silence after speaking ≠ dumb ideas — often **lack of influence skill.**
- Persuasion isn’t most liked / most talk — use **ACT:** Assert credibility → Contextualize → Trigger action.
- **Credibility:** assertive + honest limitations (nuance lowers defenses).
- **Context:** social proof (who else is on board) lowers uncertainty risk.
- **Action:** easy low-commitment call to action (Newton: objects in motion stay in motion) — e.g. add to draft thread.

## Interview philosophy

Being heard is influence design. Same ACT pattern works in meetings and in interview recommendations.

## Evaluation criteria

- Nuanced credibility (not arrogant certainty)
- Social proof context
- Easy next-step CTA

## Common candidate mistakes

- Chase likability → ignored
- Practice speech but no influence structure
- “I promise this is great” with no who-else proof
- No easy action ask after the idea

## Follow-up questions

- “Did I assert credibility with honest limits?”
- “Who else is already on board (social proof)?”
- “What’s my low-commitment trial ask?”

## Answer frameworks

### ACT

1. **Assert credibility** — confident + caveats  
2. **Contextualize** — social proof / who’s adopting  
3. **Trigger action** — easy yes (draft thread, trial)  

## Good vs bad examples

### Ignored raise-hand vs ACT

**Bad scene (verbatim):**

> I've got this thing that I really want to say… raise my hand and just say it… afterwards silence. Maybe someone says okay we'll think about it but usually people just move on

**Credibility (verbatim):**

> given the small sample size I'd caution that results aren't statistically definitive, that said we're seeing a strong directional signal that aligns with last quarter's trends. When you lead with nuance people lower their defenses

**Context bad → good (verbatim):**

> instead of saying I promise you this is a great idea say product in ops have been early adopters and feedback's been positive.

**Action (verbatim):**

> Can I add you to the draft thread so you can weigh in if you'd like? It's light easy to say yes to and suddenly they're on board

**Close (verbatim):**

> So are you chasing likeability or building influence
