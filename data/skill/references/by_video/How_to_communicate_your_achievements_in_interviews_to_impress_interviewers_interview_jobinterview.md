## Core principles

- **You have achieved impressive things** — most wins didn’t feel like wins; they felt like work (that’s why “proudest accomplishment” blanks).
- **Career CPR:** revive stories that already exist for interviews, performance reviews, promo chats.
- **Brain dump without judging** — list 3–5 years of projects; ordinary moments hide gold under imposter syndrome and excess humility.
- **Shortlist on Challenge × Validation × Impact** (H/M/L); high on all three = superstar achievement.
- **Think like a boss** to translate day-to-day work into business value — don’t wait for someone to tell you your work matters.

## Interview philosophy

Interviewers (and promo panels) don’t reward “I did my job.” They reward stories that show challenge, external validation, and measurable business impact. Candidates must reframe ordinary tasks into root-cause → action → business outcome narratives.

## Evaluation criteria

Score each story:

| Dimension | Rate | Guiding idea from transcript |
|-----------|------|------------------------------|
| **Challenge** | H/M/L | Was there real difficulty / underperformance to solve? |
| **Validation** | H/M/L | External recognition / proof beyond “manager was happy”? |
| **Impact** | H/M/L | Business value (conversion, revenue, targets)? |

Superstar = high on all three.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Believing you haven’t achieved anything impressive.
- Filtering the brain dump to only “impressive-sounding” items.
- Saying “I updated the landing page and my manager was happy” — that’s doing your job, not an achievement.
- Not knowing how day-to-day work maps to company benefit / impact.
- Waiting for someone else to declare that your work matters.

## Follow-up questions

- “For this story: Challenge / Validation / Impact — H, M, or L?”
- “What was underperforming before I acted?”
- “What root cause did I find?”
- “What did I rebuild/change?”
- “What metric moved — and what business outcome followed?”

## Answer frameworks

### Three-step career CPR

1. **Brain dump** — every project last 3–5 years; big or small; no filter; capture ordinary moments.
2. **Shortlist** — score Challenge, Validation, Impact (H/M/L); prioritize triple-high.
3. **Think like a boss** — reframe into business impact with root cause → action → outcome + numbers.

### Impact reframe pattern (from marketing analyst example)

Underperforming outcome → discovered root cause → rebuilt/fixed → metric lift → business target / revenue.

## Good vs bad examples

### Blank mind vs brain dump

**Scenario:** “What is your proudest accomplishment?” — mind goes blank.

**Why (verbatim):**

> The truth is you have achieved impressive things, but most of your wins didn't feel like wins when they happened. They just felt like work.

**Good process (verbatim):**

> Step one is your brain dump. Stop judging and start writing. Just list out every project from last three to five years. Big or small, don't filter, don't only write down the ones that sound impressive… Because these ordinary moments are where your gold is buried under your imposter syndrome and a lifetime of being too humble.

---

### Doing your job vs achievement reframe

**Scenario:** Marketing analyst coached by Jonathan.

**Bad (verbatim):**

> I updated a company websites landing page and my manager was happy and I was proud of myself. That's not an achievement. That's called doing your job.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Our product launch was underperforming. I discovered that poor landing page conversion was the root cause. So I rebuilt the page from the ground up to better articulate our value proposition. It lifted conversion by 1%, helped us reach our launch target, and drove $5 million incremental revenue in the first year. Same experience, completely different impact.

**Close (verbatim):**

> So stop waiting for someone to tell you that your work matters. Start finding out how for yourself.
