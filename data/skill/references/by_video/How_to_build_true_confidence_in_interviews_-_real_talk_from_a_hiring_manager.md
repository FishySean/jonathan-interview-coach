## Core principles

- **There are no adults in the interview room** — both sides are "scared kids in business suits."
- **Interviewers remember ~10% of what you say** — they're overwhelmed, often unprepared; make that 10% count.
- **Carry high-value confidence** — attitude: "I'm a high value candidate and you'd be lucky if I accept your offer."
- **Every interview boils down to three questions:** (1) Are you the best candidate? (2) Is this the best job for you? (3) Would I like working with you?
- **Stories stick; facts don't.** Clear structure beats perfect English/grammar.
- **Unexpected questions: collaborate, don't freeze** — ask what something means; show you can find answers with a peer.

## Interview philosophy

Jonathan demystifies the interviewer: forgot the meeting until recruiter pinged; asks "introduce yourself" to buy time because resume unread; follow-ups often stall for note-taking. He's not grading grammar — he's tired and will check out if you ramble. Motivation matters (retention). Collaboration under uncertainty matters more than knowing everything.

## Evaluation criteria

| Meta-question | What wins | Red flags |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| Best candidate? | Clear, structured, easy to follow; interesting stories | Perfect-English obsession; rambling |
| Best job for you? | Research; look like you want *this* role most | Generic interest; risk of quitting in 6 months |
| Like working with you? | Calm, collaborative, curious when stuck | Deer-in-headlights freeze; can't problem-solve together |

Offer goes to: "no matter what happens, I trust this person to get it done."

## Common candidate mistakes

- Assuming the interviewer is fully prepared and "has it together."
- Memorizing scripts for perfect English instead of clear structure.
- Rambling until the interviewer checks out.
- Freezing on unexpected topics (e.g. AI agents) instead of clarifying and collaborating.
- Treating the interview as a test instead of joint problem-solving.

## Follow-up questions

When confidence/collaboration is weak, ask:

- "Which of the three meta-questions did that answer serve?"
- "Can you make the next 60 seconds the 10% I'd remember — with a story?"
- "You don't know X — how would you figure it out with me as a peer?"

## Answer frameworks

**Three meta-questions (map every BQ here):**
1. Best candidate → clear structure + stories
2. Best job for you → research + motivation signal
3. Like working together → calm curiosity under unknowns

**Unknown topic response:** Don't freeze — ask what it is; stay collaborative and resourceful.

## Good vs bad examples

### Interviewer side — not as in control as they look

**Jonathan as HM (verbatim):**

> As a hiring manager, I probably remember like 10% of what you say. Not because I don't care, but because I have a day job and I'm overwhelmed.

> Behind the scenes, I forgot about this interview until the recruiter pinged me. I asked you to introduce yourself to buy time because I haven't read your resume yet. And the real reason that I'm pestering you with follow-up questions? I'm stalling because I need some time to think and take notes.

**Reframe (verbatim):**

> Point is, your interviewer doesn't have their **** together either. We're all faking something as interviewers, but we still have to show up in a suit, and so do you. Not literally, but in the way that you carry yourself, with confidence and an attitude like, I'm a high value candidate and you'd be lucky if I accept your offer.

---

### Best candidate — grammar vs clarity

**Bad:** Stressing over memorized perfect-English scripts.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Stop stressing about memorizing your script with perfect English. I'm not grading your grammar. I'm barely writing things down. I just need answers that are clear, structured, and easy to follow because I'm tired, and if you ramble, then I'm going to check out.

> A guaranteed way to keep me with you is telling me interesting stories.

> Remember, story stick, facts don't.

---

### Best job for you — motivation / retention

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> You need to look like you want this role more than anyone else. Why? Because motivated people stay longer. And that's important to me because I don't want to have to interview candidates again in six months because you quit when you found out that this was not the right role for you.

---

### Like working with you — unexpected question

**Scenario:** "What's your experience with AI agents?"

**Bad (verbatim):**

> Don't freeze like a deer in the headlights.

**Good (verbatim):**

> You can just ask me what that is. If you don't know, if you're calm, collaborative, and curious, I'll almost always work with you because in the real workplace, you don't have to have all the answers, right? You just need to know how to find them.
