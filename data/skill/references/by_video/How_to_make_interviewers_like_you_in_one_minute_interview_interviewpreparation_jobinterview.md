## Core principles

- First ~30 seconds often start with **“How are you?”** — not “Tell me about yourself”; most waste it with “Fine, how are you?”
- **Three moves:** (1) say their **name** (Dale Carnegie / barber “Hey John”), (2) **prep small talk** — common ground from LinkedIn or a short relatable slice of you, (3) **smile** (even on phone — changes voice via hormones/endorphins).
- It’s not just small talk — **first impression you never get to redo.**

## Interview philosophy

Rapport is deliberate. Name + prepped common ground pulls the interviewer into an intimate space before content questions begin.

## Evaluation criteria

- Used interviewer’s name
- Specific common ground or relatable detail (not blank “fine”)
- Warm face/voice (smile)

## Common candidate mistakes

- “Fine, how are you?” and wasting tone-setting
- No small-talk prep (especially non-chatty candidates)
- Stone face / flat tone when nervous

## Follow-up questions

- “Do I know their name and will I use it?”
- “LinkedIn: same city/school/hobby for a one-liner?”
- “What’s my short ‘slice of me’ backup?”

## Answer frameworks

1. Name  
2. Common ground **or** slice of you  
3. Smile  

## Good vs bad examples

### How are you?

**Bad (verbatim):**

> fine, how are you?

**Name (verbatim teaching):**

> First, say their name… a person's own name is the sweetest sound… why I love my barber… hey, John, how's it going?

**Common ground (verbatim):**

> I noticed that you're also based in Toronto. I actually lived just a few blocks from the CN Tower. Which part of the city are you in?

**Slice of you (verbatim):**

> it feels like August is flying by already. I cannot believe it's almost Q4. Is it a busy season on your end too?

**Smile (verbatim):**

> Even in a phone call interview, smiling can change the way that your voice sounds. It's hormones and endorphins and it's just science.
