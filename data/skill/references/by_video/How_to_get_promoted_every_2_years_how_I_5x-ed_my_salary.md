## Core principles

- Companies don’t promote you because you **deserve** it — only when they **need** you (more responsibility, keep you happy, or keep you from leaving).
- Fast growers don’t wait passively — they **seek promotions outside** the company too.
- Every ~**two years**, go back to the job market — out there you control the narrative.
- If you can **convince the interviewer** you have the skills, the promotion is yours (external offer sets pace).
- Never let **one company** decide your pace; make them **compete with the market** for your talent.
- Funny pattern: own company often “recognizes” you only after another company discovers your value.
- **Never wait for permission to grow** — your promotion is already out there.

## Interview philosophy

External interviews are a promotion mechanism. Market signal > internal loyalty narrative. Jonathan: five promotions in ten years, director at 30 — not genius/harder work, but controlling pace via the market.

## Evaluation criteria

- Active external search cadence (~2 years)
- Ability to sell skills in interviews (not only internal performance)
- Willingness to use outside interest as leverage when manager asks what would make you stay

## Common candidate mistakes

- Believing hard work + boss liking you → they’ll sit you down with a promotion.
- Waiting 3–5–10 years while an outside hire takes “your” role.
- Letting company say “wait three years” set your ceiling.
- Waiting forever for internal recognition.

## Follow-up questions

Coach:

- “When did you last test your market value?”
- “Are you waiting for permission, or setting pace?”
- If manager asks what would make you stay: have the promotion ask ready.

## Answer frameworks

1. Stop passive “deserve it” waiting.
2. Build skills + interview proof.
3. Every ~2 years: run a real external process.
4. Let market competition create internal urgency.
5. When asked to stay → negotiate the promotion you’d been asking for.

## Good vs bad examples

### Passive internal myth

**Bad belief:** Work hard, boss likes me → someday “John, great job, here’s a promotion.”

**Reality Jonathan saw:** Coworkers slave 3–10 years; outside candidate hired into the deserved role.

### Manager reverse-ask (after market interest)

> John, is there anything we can do to make you stay?

> Yeah, let me think. Maybe getting me that promotion that I've been asking for.

> Oh, yeah, John, we told you that it takes three to five years. Usually, but for an exceptional talent like you, I think we could make it work if you stay.

**Why it matters:** Another company discovers value first → own company suddenly flexible.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Promotion-via-market thesis

- **Central question this Short addresses:** Get promoted every 2 years / how he 5x’d salary — market over waiting.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** Career growth / internal vs external promotion strategy (also leverage conversations with manager).
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> Companies don't promote you because you deserve it. They only promote you when they need you, need you to take more responsibility, need to keep you happy, or need you to stay. So I flipped that script. Every two years, I went back to the job market. Out there, you control the narrative. Without begging your boss to notice or waiting for a spot to open up, out there, you're in control of your career growth. It doesn't matter if your boss thinks that you're a poor performer or your company says that you need to wait three years. If you have the skills... No, actually, if you can convince your interviewer that you have the skills, then the promotion is yours. That's how I got five promotions in ten years, and I reached director level at 30. Not because I was a genius, not because I worked harder, but because I never let one company decide my pace. I set it for myself, and I made them compete with the job market for my talent at all times.

> Funny how another company has to discover your value first before your own company will suddenly recognize it. So if you've been waiting and waiting for your company to recognize you, stop. Your promotion is already out there. Never wait for permission to grow.
