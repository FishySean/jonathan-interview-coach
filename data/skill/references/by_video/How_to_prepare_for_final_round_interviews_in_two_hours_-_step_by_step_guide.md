## Core principles

- **Final-round offers hinge on values, not skills.** Skills already cleared earlier rounds.
- **Final-round leader's real question:** Who are you and do you have a future here?
- **VEA inventory** ties identity to results: Values → Experiences → Achievements (leaders are results-oriented).
- **Executive communication improves by communicating more** — practice out loud (AI voice mode works when you have no partner / weak feedback).
- **Don't ask junior repeat questions** in the final round; ask investor-level questions (strategy, positioning, vision).

## Interview philosophy

Jonathan maps the funnel: HR (qualified) → hiring manager (meets the bar) → final leader (fit + future). Final prep is a timed SOP (~2 hours): short executive-comms mock, deep VEA values work, then high-level questions. Even "soft" prompts (e.g. favorite season) become values + story + achievement if VEA is ready.

## Evaluation criteria

Score whether the candidate:

1. **Communicates at executive level** — Clear essence; structured via a leadership framework (pyramid principle / SEQA / bluff / similar)
2. **Shows values with proof** — Value → experience story → resulting achievement
3. **Answers "who are you / future here"** — Not just skill recitation
4. **Asks investor-grade questions** — Strategy, unique positioning, vision — not culture / day-in-the-life repeats

Red flags: same junior questions as earlier rounds; freezing on values-probing soft questions; unstructured leadership answers.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Treating final round like another skills screen.
- Practicing polished scripts instead of natural out-loud delivery first.
- Asking "What is your company culture?" or "What does the day look like?" again.
- Listing values without experiences and achievements that prove them.
- Freezing on unexpected personal questions because VEA wasn't prepared.

## Follow-up questions

When final-round prep is weak, ask:

- "Who are you — and why do you have a future here?"
- "Which value are you leading with, and what's the experience + achievement that proves it?"
- "Can you reconstruct that answer with pyramid principle / SEQA / bluff?"
- "If you were an investor in this company, what would you ask about strategy / positioning / vision?"
- "What's your natural (unscripted) answer to 'What's your leadership style?' — then what's the structured version?"

## Answer frameworks

**~2-hour final-round SOP:**

| Block | Time | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive communication mock | 4–5 min | AI voice mode → answer leadership Q out loud (no script) → AI summarize essence → AI reconstruct with a leadership framework |
| Values analysis (VEA) | ~60 min | Build Values / Experiences / Achievements inventory |
| Prepare good questions | ~15 min | Investor-level questions on strategy, unique positioning, vision |

**AI practice loop:**
1. Open AI in voice mode
2. Answer a common leadership question out loud — naturally, no script, no translating in your head (e.g. "What's your leadership style?")
3. Ask AI to summarize what you said (see the essence)
4. Ask AI to reconstruct using a leadership communication framework (pyramid principle, SEQA, bluff — whatever resonates; they're similar)

**VEA inventory columns:**
1. **Values** — list in first column
2. **Experiences** — story where you showed that value (work preferred; life OK)
3. **Achievements** — result of that experience (ties who you are to what you can achieve)

**Question posture:** Imagine yourself as the **investor** of the company; ask what leaders ask themselves (strategy, unique positioning, vision).

## Good vs bad examples

### Final-round decision logic

**Jonathan's funnel (verbatim):**

> The truth is, in final rounds, your skills won't get you the offer. Your values are what will give you the offer. HR decided that you're qualified in the first round. The hiring manager decided you meet the bar in the second round. But in the final round, the leader is asking one question. Who are you and do you have a future here?

---

### Soft question: "What's your favorite season?" (VEA in action)

**Scenario:** Investment banking interview; MD asks a personal question.

**Bad:** Freeze (Jonathan: "If you're like most people, you might freeze this question").

**Good — coached candidate answer (verbatim as Jonathan recounts):**

> She said, my favorite season is winter. Empathy has always been a core value for me and I love helping others during the holiday season. Last December, I invited my colleagues to join me in volunteering at the food bank and I organized a day trip, which helped us to bond as a team and help the community. I was really proud to see that my team actually highlighted my initiative on our social responsibility webpage.

**Structure inside the answer:** value (empathy) → experience (food bank day trip) → achievement (highlighted on social responsibility webpage).

---

### Final questions: junior vs investor

**Bad (verbatim examples Jonathan rejects):**

> what is your company culture or what does the day look like?

**Why bad (verbatim):**

> Please don't show how junior you are by asking the same questions that you've asked in previous rounds

**Good posture (verbatim):**

> Instead, you have to imagine yourself as the investor of this company and ask them some high level questions. You know, the kind of questions that they ask themselves all the time. Ask them about their strategy, ask them about their unique positioning, ask them about their vision for the company.
