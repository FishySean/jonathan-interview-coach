## Core principles

- 如果你在面试里用“先做了这个、再做了那个”的时间线讲故事，你已经失去他们的注意力。
- 注意力不是你“应得”的，是你需要通过讲故事去 **earn** 的。
- 让面试官记住你的三个关键动作：
  1. 用 **vivid hook**（时间+场景+情绪）开场
  2. 总结你 **unique approach**（对比、心智模型、golden sentence 三选一）
  3. 用 **reflection**（你如何改变）收尾，并且要和你申请的岗位关联

## Interview philosophy

面试官不是历史学家，而是决策者；他们不会记住一串事实时间线，而会被“能让他们共情并形成决定的叙事”记住（包括神经耦合 neural coupling）。

## Evaluation criteria

- 开头 hook 是否生动（time + scene + emotion）
- 你是否把“你怎么想”的 playbook 表达出来（而不是只列动作）
- reflection 是否解释“你如何成长/变化”，并回扣岗位

## Common candidate mistakes

- 过度按时间顺序复述经历（chronology）
- 不解释你的战略/心智模型，只讲做了什么
- 只说结果没有变化，没有 tie back 到岗位

## Follow-up questions

- 我有没有用时间+场景+情绪做开场 hook？
- 我有没有选择一个方式总结 unique approach（contrast / mental model / golden sentence）？
- 我有没有用反思收尾，并且明确与目标岗位相关？

## Answer frameworks

### Three-part story formula

1. **Open with a vivid hook**
2. **Summarize your unique approach**
3. **End with a reflection tied to the job**

### Unique approach（3 个选项）

- Contrast yourself
- Name your mental model（如 80-20）
- Create your own golden sentence

## Good vs bad examples

### Bad：时间线复述 vs Good：讲给人共情的故事

**Bad（verbatim framing）**：

> chronology explains, but good storytelling persuades.

**Good teaching（verbatim）**：

> This works because our brains are wired for stories, not lists of facts.

### Hook 的 verbatim 示例（verbatim）

> It's Friday, 9am. I walk into the office and the whole floor is frozen. Our e-commerce site is down, no sales, no revenue. And everyone's staring at me, the only developer in the room.

### Unique approach（verbatim：contrast / mental model / golden sentence）

> You could say, most people would try to research and come up with a smart idea. But instead, I identified who was missing from the brainstorm invite and made sure the right voices were in the room.

> Or you could name your mental model, like I always use the 80-20 principle.

> Jingzhu, like when I'm faced with a problem, I don't try to be the smartest person in the room. I make sure that I bring the smartest voices into the room.

### Reflection tie-back（verbatim）

> That experience taught me that chaos isn't noise. It's signal. My role is to step up and separate the two so that my team can focus on solutions.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### Whole “remembered story” outline（sample from transcript）

- **Central question this Short addresses:** 如何让面试官真正记住你（不是时间线）
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** behavioral / “tell me about a time…” / any story question
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> If you want interviewers to actually remember you, you need to do these three things. First, open your story with a vivid hook. You can use this formula, time plus scene plus emotion, like this.

> It's Friday, 9am. I walk into the office and the whole floor is frozen. Our e-commerce site is down, no sales, no revenue. And everyone's staring at me, the only developer in the room.

> Next, summarize your unique approach.

> Instead of telling the interviewer, oh yeah, on the top candidate, you're showing it to them through your deeper level of insight and strategy.

> Finally, you want to end your story with a reflection.

> That experience taught me that chaos isn't noise. It's signal. My role is to step up and separate the two so that my team can focus on solutions.

> Remember, your interviewer is not a historian. They're a decision maker.

> So don't give them a timeline. Give them a reason to care.
