## Core principles

- You can’t become the most qualified overnight — you **can** act like it and pass first round with a **2-hour SOP**.
- AI gathers info; **only you** make sense of it.
- Career story needs a **thread** tied to a core JD competency (especially if you’re not the ideal profile).
- Prep the **20% of questions that drive 80%** of first-round success.
- Dry run: memorize **beats** (ideas/keywords/emotions), not word-for-word — sound real, not GPT-recited.

## Interview philosophy

First-round prep = learn the dream-candidate character → become them via a logical career thread → draft core scripts → practice in your own words. Sleep + prepare earlier next time.

## Evaluation criteria

(For self-prep quality)

- Dream persona from JD + pain points
- HM lens: why top competencies matter
- One-sentence company money model
- LinkedIn peers: shared strengths + gaps
- Career thread into this interview
- Beats-based dry run until confident

## Common candidate mistakes

- Skim JD, highlight keywords, hope.
- Letting AI “think” for you.
- No logical thread if pivoting (e.g. eng → PM).
- Trying to perfect every question instead of the vital 20%.
- Memorizing scripts word-for-word.
- Chasing perfect English over sounding real.

## Follow-up questions

Self-check during prep:

- “If I were the hiring manager, why would these five competencies matter the most to me?”
- “How do they help my team succeed?”

## Answer frameworks

### 2-hour first-round SOP

| Block | Time | Goal |
|-------|------|------|
| 1 | 30 min | Learn the character: AI JD → persona/pain; internalize; company $ one-liner; LinkedIn peer compare |
| 2 | 30 min | Become the character: one career thread → this interview |
| 3 | 30 min | Draft core first-round question scripts (20/80) |
| 4 | 30 min | Dry run: speak without script → check misses → repeat |

## Good vs bad examples

### Career thread (eng → PM)

**Good (verbatim):**

> I've always loved solving problems, which is why I joined Company A as an engineer. But after working closely with our PM, I realized that my favorite problems actually involve people, not code. I wanted to work more closely to solve problems for users, and that's why I'm here today.

### Dry run

**Bad:** Memorize word for word.

**Good:** Memorize beats; throw away script; speak; suck once; compare; repeat until confident in your own words.
