## Core principles

- **Worse than minimum wage: a boss who gaslights you into believing you deserve it.**
- **Your labor is as cheap as you believe it is** in this market — belief shapes the price you accept.
- **Companies that exploit employees stay small:** “If you pay the minimum, you get the minimum” — and become the minimum version of what they could have been.
- **Breaking out is possible** — leave exploitative environments; ask for what you deserve.
- **Flip the boss’s slogan into motivation:** “Your price does reflect your value” → use that to demand fair pay.

## Interview philosophy

This Short is career dignity / compensation mindset more than interview technique. Jonathan coaches professionals (especially in small Chinese companies in the US) who absorb demeaning “value = price” rhetoric while underpaid. Market proof (mainstream offer >2×) is the antidote; then pay it forward by telling coworkers to think bigger.

## Evaluation criteria

Not interviewer scorecards — self/market evaluation:

- **Dignity baseline:** Education, drive, and humanity already exceed minimum-wage gaslighting.
- **Market signal:** Verbal offer at a mainstream company at more than double current pay = evidence the old “price” was exploitation, not true value.
- **Company quality filter:** Exploiters stay small; underpaying produces underperformance as an org.

## Common candidate mistakes

- Internalizing a boss’s gaslighting (“你的价值决定了你的价格”) while paid minimum wage.
- Staying in exploitative small-company cultures that normalize stepping on people.
- Believing your labor must stay cheap because the current employer says so.
- Not seeking mainstream-market options that price you correctly.

## Follow-up questions

Coach / self-check:

- “Am I accepting pay because I believe I deserve it — or because someone gaslit me into it?”
- “What would a mainstream-market offer say about my price vs this employer’s story?”
- “Am I telling coworkers (and myself) to think bigger — or reinforcing the BS?”
- “If my price reflects my value, what action do I take this week to ask for what I deserve?”

## Answer frameworks

Jonathan’s reframes (usable in negotiation mindset / exit conversations):

1. **Gaslight recognition:** Minimum wage + “your value determines your price” = exploitation narrative, not objective truth.
2. **Market proof:** Seek/land mainstream offers that reset the price (her case: more than double).
3. **Org economics:** Pay minimum → get minimum → company stays minimum.
4. **Motivation flip:** Accept “price reflects value” as *reason to ask for more*, not reason to stay underpaid.

## Good vs bad examples

### Boss gaslighting vs human dignity

**Scenario:** Candidate at a small Chinese company in the US; Ivy League master’s; boss messages “你的价值决定了你的价格” while paying minimum wage.

**Bad (boss / exploiters — verbatim framing):**

> Her boss says things like, 你的价值决定了你的价格 while paying her minimum wage… And yet some people actively choose to exploit others and step on them laughing while they do it.

**Good (Jonathan’s humanizing pushback — verbatim):**

> You know, she has a master's degree from an Ivy League school. She's smart, driven, and above all, she's someone's daughter. You know, a human being. No, I don't know what her value is to her loved ones or herself, but it's certainly more than a minimum wage. I think we can agree on that… It costs zero dollars to be kind, right?

---

### Staying trapped vs breaking out and paying it forward

**Scenario:** Same candidate after coaching / job search.

**Good outcome (verbatim):**

> She's moving on. She has a verbal offer at a mainstream company and they're paying her more than double what she's making now. And now that she broke out of the POA and found something better on the other side, well, she's telling her coworkers to think bigger and reject the utter BS that's being shoveled at them.

**Company side (verbatim):**

> Because companies who exploit their employees will always stay small. Because if you pay the minimum, you get the minimum. And you become the minimum version of what you could have been as a company.

---

### Reframe of the boss’s slogan

**Scenario:** Viewer still underpriced by belief.

**Bad internalization:** Treating the boss’s “value → price” line as permission to stay cheap.

**Good (verbatim flip):**

> In this market, your labor is as cheap as you believe it is. So you know what? I guess that manager was right. Your price does reflect your value. Make that the motivation to ask for what you deserve.
