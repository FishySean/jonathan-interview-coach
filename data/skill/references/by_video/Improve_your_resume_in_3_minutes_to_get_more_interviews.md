## Core principles

- **A good resume does three things:** (1) story-driven bullets, (2) remove red flags, (3) avoid bad formatting — formatting is especially important because bad format = reject in ~5 seconds.
- **Responsibilities don't differentiate you** — show business impact via stories, not random numbers labeled "achievement."
- **Effort is cheap; impact is what matters to HR.**
- **Readers skim asking:** "Is there any reason I shouldn't interview this person?"
- **Resume is not a passport** — experience can speak louder than address/location lines.

## Interview philosophy

Resume screening is negative selection under volume (~200 resumes). Jonathan optimizes for: impact stories that survive a skim, eliminating easy reject reasons, and ATS-friendly simple formatting. Quantified impact can be estimated — "they're not gonna call your previous manager" about 21% vs 22%.

## Evaluation criteria

Score a resume on:

1. **Story bullets** — Skill + action + result; impact areas: ops efficiency, cost savings, revenue
2. **Red-flag absence** — missing JD skills, no relevant experience story, location bias signals
3. **Formatting** — one-column B&W; ≥10pt; ≤1 page if <10 years exp; clean filename

## Common candidate mistakes

- Responsibility-only bullets; numbers without a story.
- Missing JD-required skills → instant screen-out.
- Fancy designer layouts that break ATS and frustrate recruiters.
- Tiny fonts; multi-page resumes for junior/mid careers.
- Leaving China locations if that triggers bias (his advice: remove locations).
- Forgetting filename as first impression.

## Follow-up questions

- "Where's skill + action + result in this bullet?"
- "Which of ops efficiency / cost / revenue does this hit?"
- "What would make a skimmer say 'don't interview' — and did we remove it?"
- "Would this survive ATS + 5-second glance?"

## Answer frameworks

**Golden formula:** Skill + action + result

**Impact focus areas:** operational efficiency, cost savings, revenue increase

**Three tips:**
1. Story-driven points
2. Remove red flags (skills JIT; build experience via project sites; consider dropping locations)
3. Avoid bad formatting (simple 1-column B&W; ≥10pt; one page if <10 YOE)

## Good vs bad examples

### Story-driven bullet

**Bad (verbatim-ish example he gives):**

> Used Python to clean data pipelines and reduce reporting time by 40%

**Good (verbatim):**

> Proactively resolved client reporting complaints by building a data cleaning pipeline with Python, automated validation from disparate data sources, reducing manual reporting time by 40%.

**Why (verbatim):**

> Most resumes focus on responsibilities, but responsibilities don't differentiate you at all. … And no, that doesn't mean adding some numbers and calling it an achievement. You actually have to tell a story.

---

### Quantification

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> Now, if you can't quantify an achievement exactly, no worries. Just estimate it. They're not gonna call your previous manager up to ask if you increased efficiency by 21% or 22%, right?

> Remember that effort is cheap, but impact is what matters to HR.

---

### Red flag — missing skill (SQL)

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> While they skim your resume, every reader is asking themselves, is there any reason I shouldn't interview this person?

> Like if they need you to know SQL and you know nothing about it, I'm gonna be real with you, okay? Just put it on your resume first, then go learn it. … If you don't write that skill down, you're gonna get screened out like that. So take a just-in-time approach to skills. If you're missing them, go learn them and write them down.

---

### No relevant experience / China locations

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> Or what if you don't have relevant work experience? No problem, go get it for yourself right now. There are sites that are built for this. You don't need a full-time job to start building experience.

> Or what if all of your work experience is in China? No problem, remove the locations from your resume. Your resume is not a passport. Let your experiences speak louder than your address.

---

### Formatting rejects

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> Good formatting doesn't automatically get you hired, but bad formatting will get you rejected in the first five seconds.

Three no-pile mistakes:
1. Fancy designer formats → kill ATS; frustrating to read → **simple one column black and white text**
2. Tiny text → **10 point or above** (HR won't zoom)
3. Too long → **under 10 years experience → one page usually enough**
