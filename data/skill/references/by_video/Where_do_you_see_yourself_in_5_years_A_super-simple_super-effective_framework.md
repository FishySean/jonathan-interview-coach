## Core principles

- **The bolder your title vision, the bigger the red flag** — crystal-ball “I’ll be a director” ≈ first-date “marry in two years.”
- Vague “I just want to grow” is also weak.
- **Best candidates focus on controllable inputs** (grow, contribute, lead) — not uncontrollable outcomes (job titles).
- **SUPREME / Skills → Impact → People** (he says “supreme work first as skills…” — ASR; structure is Skills / Impact / People with timelines):
  - **Skills** (3–6 months): foundation for high performance; learn fast, add value early
  - **Impact** (1–2 years): results — projects, efficiency, cross-team wins
  - **People** (5 years): mentoring, leading, collaborating — presence scales beyond yourself
- **Don’t point at a destination — build the vehicle.** Don’t say you’ll be a director; show what you’ll do to earn it.

## Interview philosophy

“Where do you see yourself in 5 years?” tests maturity and controllability. Practical timelines + skill/impact/people arcs beat vague growth or premature title claims.

## Evaluation criteria

- Controllable inputs vs title fantasy
- Specific skills early; outcomes mid; influence later
- Company-tied growth (“grow with Acme Inc”)
- Practical goal-setting signal

## Common candidate mistakes

- Vague: “I just want to grow”
- Over-bold title prediction (“I’m gonna be a director one day”)
- Focusing on outcomes you can’t control
- Low-performer pattern: point at destination and promise arrival without the vehicle

## Follow-up questions

- “What skills in 3–6 months show early value?”
- “What impact in 1–2 years proves learning → outcomes?”
- “How does my year-5 ‘people’ influence scale beyond myself?”
- “Am I naming a title I can’t control, or inputs I can?”

## Answer frameworks

### Skills → Impact → People (timeline)

1. **3–6 months — Skills:** deepen X to support Y early  
2. **1–2 years — Impact:** lead/own Z; end-to-end outcomes  
3. **Year 5 — People:** mentor; build best practices; strategic not just reactive

**Teaching (verbatim):**

> the best candidates they don't focus on outcomes that they cannot control like job titles they focus on the inputs that they can control like how they grow how they contribute how they lead

> low performers they point at a destination in the future and promise that they'll get there but high performers will they actually build a vehicle to get there so don't tell them that you'll be a director one day show them what you're willing to do to earn it
