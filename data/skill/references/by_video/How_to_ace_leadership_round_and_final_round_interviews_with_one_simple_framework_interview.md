## Core principles

- **Final / leadership rounds fail when you can’t prove:** “Can you grow to become a leader at this company one day?”
- **VA system:** Values → Experience → Achievement (share core beliefs; story where you lived the value; end with impact).
- **Values prep is trainable** even if you “don’t know what you’re having for dinner.”
- **Overlap company values with yours** — then map human (not overly technical) stories and impact on self/others.
- **Integrity stories** that connect life → career → company value (e.g. “trust first”) land with leaders.

## Interview philosophy

Leadership interviews test future leadership potential, not only past IC craft. Leaders listen for lived values that match culture — proven through human stories and impact — more than dense technical detail.

## Evaluation criteria

What final-round leaders probe:

- **Growth-to-leader signal:** Can this person become a leader here someday?
- **Value clarity:** Do you have core beliefs at work *and* in life?
- **Lived proof:** Experience story where you actually lived that value.
- **Impact close:** Achievement / effect on yourself and those around you.
- **Culture fit:** Overlap with company values (culture / careers / about page).

## Common candidate mistakes

- Failing finals because they never prove leadership growth potential.
- Not knowing personal values — and not fixing that before the interview.
- Overly detailed/technical stories instead of human stories.
- No mapping between personal values and company values.
- Skipping the impact ending after stating a value.

## Follow-up questions

Prep / coach:

- “Which of my top values overlap with this company’s stated values?”
- “For each overlapping value, what’s one life/career story where I lived it?”
- “How did my actions impact myself and people around me?”
- “If asked ‘Who do you look up to?’, does my answer run Values → Experience → Achievement and land on their culture word?”

## Answer frameworks

### VA (Values → Experience → Achievement)

1. **Values** — share core beliefs not only at work, but in life.
2. **Experience** — tell a story where you lived that value.
3. **Achievement** — end with the impact you created.

### Three prep steps

1. Take a free quick values assessment; shortlist top 10 values.
2. Check company culture / careers / about page; find three values that overlap or relate to yours.
3. Map three experiences demonstrating each value (human stories); identify impact on self and others.

## Good vs bad examples

### Not knowing values vs solving it in three steps

**Scenario:** Candidate protests they don’t even know dinner plans, let alone values.

**Bad / stuck (verbatim tone):**

> uh, John, I don't even know what I'm having for dinner tonight. How am I supposed to know my values?

**Good process (verbatim):**

> First, go to this website. Take the free quick assessment and shortlist your top 10 values… So next we're going to check the company's culture or careers page or about his page and find three values that overlap or are related to our values. Then map three experiences in your life and career where you demonstrated each value. Avoid getting too detailed or technical here. Remember, these are human stories. And finally, identify how your actions impacted yourself and those around you.

## Jonathan's sample answers (verbatim)

### “Who do you look up to?” (coached candidate reply)

- **Central question this Short addresses:** How to ace leadership / final rounds by proving you can grow into a leader — via VA.
- **Interview question(s) this sample answers:** “Who do you look up to?”
- **Jonathan's sample answer (verbatim):**

> I look up to my father. He lives every day with integrity, doing the right thing, even when nobody's watching. When I was 12, he was laid off from his job. I remember how stressful it was for our family, but instead of taking shortcuts or blaming others, he spent months doing free community consulting work just to keep building skills and keep giving back. I remember watching him in a small office, the way that his face would light up when he helped small businesses to grow. Later in my own career, when I faced pressure to brush over a mistake that we had made on a client project, I remembered him. I stood my ground, raised her mistake transparently to the client, and we not only kept them, but we built a longer term partnership. For me, that's what integrity means. And it's why your company's value of trust first resonates with me so deeply.

- **Notes:** Keep “raised her mistake” as in transcript (likely ASR for “our”); do not “correct” inventively.
