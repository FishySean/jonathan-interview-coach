## Core principles

- **Worst career mistake:** wasting your best years growing someone else's career.
- **Clarity comes from within** — not hunting passion "out there": know **skills, passion, and fit**.
- **Evidence-based skills** > guessing what you're good at.
- **Big Five > MBTI** for scientifically validated personality insight (even if "more boring").
- **Personality assists or resists skills** — that tells you what you can improve until unstoppable.
- **Passion = energy + engagement evidence** (Good Time Journal + AEIOU), not what you "should" enjoy.
- **Fit** = map skills + passion to job titles/paths (AI can help generate options once you have inputs).

## Interview philosophy

Career discovery is upstream of interview answers. Candidates who only chase external prestige (parents said "writers don't make money" → "business strategy") may spend decades misaligned. Jonathan's frame: design a path that fits *you* (he returned to writing after 20+ years).

## Evaluation criteria

For career-discovery coaching, score whether the person has:

1. **Evidence-based skills** from 5 real projects that produced results
2. **Big Five × skill matrix** (assistance vs resistance per skill)
3. **Passion logs** (engagement/energy) with AEIOU specificity
4. **Fit hypotheses** (titles, salary/progression, how skills/passion map)

## Common candidate mistakes

- Guessing strengths instead of mining result-producing skills from real projects.
- Using MBTI as identity instead of Big Five for skill fit.
- Confusing "should enjoy" activities with ones that actually give energy.
- Looking for direction only outside (job posts, others' careers) without skills/passion clarity.

## Follow-up questions

- "Name five projects and only the skills that produced real results."
- "For each skill: does your Big Five assist or resist it?"
- "What drained you that you thought you should love — and what secretly energized you?"
- "Which titles fit both your evidence skills and energy sources?"

## Answer frameworks

**Three pillars:** Skills → Passion → Fit

**Skills:**
1. List 5 real projects
2. Write only skills that produced actual results (= evidence-based skills)
3. Take Big Five quiz
4. Per skill ask: Does my personality provide **assistance or resistance**?

Examples he gives:
- High conscientiousness → assistance for project management (naturally reliable)
- Low agreeableness → resistance for people management (draining even if competent)
- High openness → assistance for data analysis (more perspectives in data)

**Passion (Good Time Journal — Designing Your Life):**
- 3 weeks: log activities; note engagement + energy
- When energized/engaged, apply **AEIOU**: Activities, Environments, Interactions, Objects, Users

**Fit:** Feed skills + passion into AI → top 5 titles, role breakdown, salary, progression, fit insights

## Good vs bad examples

### External dream-killing vs internal clarity

**Scenario:** Childhood dream vs "practical" path.

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> When I was a kid, I wanted to be a writer. Well, my parents told me writers don't make any money. So that was the end of that dream. You know what does make money? Business strategy. Whatever that means. So that's what I did when I grew up. And it took me over two decades to realize that it wasn't my dream.

**Reframe (verbatim):**

> And no, I didn't achieve this by finding my passion from the outside. I got clarity from within by knowing three things, my skills, my passion, and my fit.

---

### Guessing skills vs evidence-based

**Bad (verbatim):**

> For this, most people just guess what they're good at.

**Good (verbatim):**

> Instead, look at five real projects that you've done. Write down only the skills that produced actual results. These are your evidence-based skills.

---

### MBTI vs Big Five

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> Forget MBTI. I know it's fun to say I'm an INTJ, but our personalities are actually a lot more complicated. The big five is the most scientifically validated personality model in psychology, even if it's a bit more boring.

**Key question (verbatim):**

> Does my personality provide assistance or resistance to the skill? Like does it help me or hurt me?

---

### Passion surprises

**Jonathan (verbatim):**

> You will notice something surprising. The things that you should enjoy often drain you. And the things that you secretly love, they give you energy every time. That's your passion in its rawest, most honest form.
