## Core principles

- **Record and review** — one habit that dramatically improves post-interview performance.
- Record answers as close to real as possible (mock or actual video interview).
- Magic is the review most people skip — **three passes:** audio only → video only → transcript as interviewer.
- You thought you were bad at interviews — really you never learned how; **stop seeking confidence, start collecting evidence.**

## Interview philosophy

Improvement is empirical. Separate channels (sound / body / text) reveal fillers, pace, fidgets, eye contact, structure, missing impact.

## Evaluation criteria

| Pass | Catch |
|------|--------|
| Audio only | Fillers, speeding when nervous, voice waver |
| Video only | Fidgets, chair turns, frown, eye contact gaps |
| Transcript | Structure, logic, ramble, fluff, missing impact |

## Common candidate mistakes

- Watch once, cringe, stop
- Never hear yourself → speaking never improves
- Seeking confidence without evidence loops

## Follow-up questions

- “Did I do all three review passes?”
- “What’s one filler / fidget / structure fix from this recording?”

## Answer frameworks

### Record & review SOP

1. Record real/mock interview answers  
2. Audio-only pass  
3. Video-only pass  
4. Transcribe + read as interviewer  

## Good vs bad examples

### Skip review vs three angles

**Process (verbatim):**

> First step, record yourself answering real interview questions… as close to the real thing as possible. Then… You don't just watch it once and cringe. You review it from three completely different angles.

**Audio (verbatim):**

> Without visuals, you're going to catch… The filler words. The way that you speed up when you're nervous. How your voice wavers when you're unsure.

**Video (verbatim):**

> I'm fidgeting with my necklace a lot and turning back and forth in my chair… frown aggressively… I didn't make eye contact with my interviewer for a whole two minutes.

**Text (verbatim):**

> Is your answer structured? Does your line of thinking sound logical… spot rambling, vague phrasing, or repeated fluff… Or… missing like your impact.

**Close (verbatim):**

> You thought that you were just bad at interviews, but really you never learned how to interview. So stop seeking more confidence and start collecting more evidence.
